# How collecting societies in Latin America, Asia, the Middle East and Africa monitor music in venues and broadcast

Research date: 2026-11-22. Budget-limited pass (about 20 tool calls). Several primary CMO pages could not be fetched: ECAD FAQ returned 403, JASRAC returned 503, Music Ally returned 429. Where a fact comes only from a search-engine summary of a page, it is marked "(snippet)". Vendor marketing claims are marked "(vendor claim)".

## Q1. Inventory: which societies monitor venues, and how

### Takeaway
Only a handful of societies in comparable markets do systematic venue monitoring with devices. Brazil's ECAD has the longest-running programme: staff place digital recorders in venues, and the recordings feed a statistical sample. Colombia's OSA (SAYCO-ACINPRO) and Mexico's SACM reportedly use Monitor Latino's discreet in-venue ambient-audio capture boxes. Japan's JASRAC trialled Audoo's ambient Audio Meter from December 2021. Peru's APDAYC said in 2018 that it was starting to install devices in venues. Most African CMOs, SONACAM included, monitor broadcast at best, with radio fingerprinting from BMAT, ACRCloud or Radiomonitor, and pay out venue money through proxies or flat sums. I found no confirmed African CMO deployment of in-venue recognition boxes.

### Cited Findings

**Brazil (ECAD)**
- In venues with live music or background music, "songs are captured through recordings made by digital devices fixed in the venue by our staff"; event organisers can also send set lists — [ECAD FAQ](https://www4.ecad.org.br/faq/como-o-ecad-sabe-quais-musicas-sao-tocadas/) (snippet; page returned 403); [ECAD events FAQ](https://www4.ecad.org.br/faq/como-o-ecad-sabe-quais-musicas-foram-tocadas-nesses-eventos-2/)
- For festas juninas and year-end parties, a work earns only if a paid-up user plays it and ECAD captures the play, or the play is reported in promoter spreadsheets or set lists — [ECAD events FAQ](https://www4.ecad.org.br/faq/como-o-ecad-sabe-quais-musicas-foram-tocadas-nesses-eventos-2/) (snippet)
- Background music in bars and restaurants is distributed quarterly by a statistical sample certified by Ibope — [ECAD Distribuição](https://www4.ecad.org.br/distribuicao/) (snippet). The certification dates to 2012–13 — [O que o Brasil ouve](https://www4.ecad.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/O-que-o-Brasil-Ouve-10-anos-de-musica.pdf) (snippet)
- The sample is 50,000 plays recorded in paying establishments — [ABRAMUS Pagamentos](https://www.abramus.org.br/musica/pagamentos/) (snippet). This conflicts with a sample of 25,000 plays certified by Ibope Inteligência in [Mundo da Música](https://mundodamusicamm.com.br/ecad-mudanca-distribuicao-musica-ambiente/)
- ECAD's tools are Ecad.Tec Som (digital recording equipment) and the automated Ecad.Tec CIA Rádio — search summary of ECAD documents (snippet; exact page not confirmed)
- The December 2025 Distribution Regulation keeps quarterly indirect distribution by statistical sample from ECAD recordings — [Regulamento de Distribuição dez/2025](https://www4.ecad.org.br/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Regulamento-de-Distribuicao-dez25.pdf) (snippet)
- Since January 2026, ECAD runs a direct distribution for part of background music, using usage reports from users and partner background-music companies. The first round covered about 90M plays in more than 3,500 stores (Cacau Show, C&A) and over 4,600 rightsholders, 60% Brazilian. The sampling model is phased out gradually — [Mundo da Música](https://mundodamusicamm.com.br/ecad-mudanca-distribuicao-musica-ambiente/)
- Academic critique: ECAD does not know exactly what plays in each establishment and distributes by statistical sample, whereas fingerprinting would allow precise identification. The same talk mentions a theme park that switched to AI-generated music and sued to avoid paying ECAD — [GEDAI/UFPR, XX CODAIP](https://gedai.ufpr.br/xx-codaip-guilherme-coutinho/)

### Inferences
- **Ambient (mic) monitoring does exist in comparable markets.** ECAD's staff-placed recorders are effectively ambient sampling, though possibly with manual or partly automated identification. Monitor Latino's discreet boxes in Colombia and Mexico, and Audoo in Japan, are ambient fingerprinting. None of these confirmed cases plugs into the sound system. This validates SoundProof's design, but Monitor Latino's 99% claim is unaudited.
- **ECAD's model is the closest template for SONACAM.** It uses a sample of venues, recordings feed a statistically certified sample, and distribution is quarterly. ECAD is now moving toward direct reports from background-music providers. Cameroon's bars mostly use DJs, USB sticks and phones rather than B2B music providers, so that path is less relevant there, which makes ambient sampling more relevant.
- Chile's SCD shows the economic counterargument: monitoring cost can exceed venue revenue. SoundProof's cost per box has to be judged against SONACAM's small envelopes (40M FCFA total in October 2025).
- Statistical certification by a third party (as with Ibope) gave ECAD sampling legitimacy. SONACAM could seek an equivalent independent validation (a university or statistics institute), given its trust deficit.
- In African CMOs, the bottleneck has often been governance (Kenya, Nigeria, Cameroon) rather than technology. Any monitoring system should publish its data, such as play counts per work, to rebuild trust.
- Francophone precedent: BURIDA already combines BMAT broadcast monitoring, venue stickers, a venue census and ERP automation. It is a natural regional comparator and possible partner for SONACAM; BUMDA, BURIDA and SODAV are signing reciprocal deals.
- Vendors already in African broadcast monitoring (ACRCloud, BMAT, Radiomonitor, Gocreate, Qisimah) could supply the fingerprint database or matching backend for low-cost venue boxes. ACRCloud's database (150M+ tracks) may still be weak on local Cameroonian repertoire, so SONACAM's own catalogue ingestion would be critical. This is inference, not verified.

## Q2. Vendors active in these markets, and reported accuracy problems

### Takeaway
BMAT has broadcast contracts in Latin America and francophone Africa: APDAYC, ACAM, and BURIDA since 2017. ACRCloud powers African radio monitoring (ZAMCOPS 2025, GHAMRO reportedly, Qisimah). Radiomonitor covers South Africa. Monitor Latino is the only vendor found selling in-venue ambient boxes in Latin America. Audoo is the main ambient-meter vendor, with JASRAC as its Asian CMO client. Gocreate in Nigeria and Qisimah in Ghana are African startups. No published independent accuracy audits were found.

### Cited Findings
- BMAT monitors more than 5,500 sources, including venues — [Wikipedia: BMAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMAT_Music_Company) (snippet)
- Music Business Worldwide notes that bars and clubs are noisy, which makes clean capture difficult, and that buyers should look past marketing hype — [MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/there-is-a-real-need-to-look-behind-the-marketing-hype-and-truly-understand-what-is-being-offered/) (snippet)
- Comparable Northern practice: PRS uses MRT devices that fingerprint DJ sets, and GEMA uses a monitoring box in clubs that identifies tracks by acoustic fingerprint — [PRS help](https://help.prsformusic.com/s/article/how-do-you-use-music-recognition-technology-to-track-dj-performances); [GEMA monitoring](https://www.gema.de/en/music-creators/monitoring)
- Vendor findings for Monitor Latino, Audoo, ACRCloud, BMAT, Gocreate and Radiomonitor are cited under Q1.

### Inferences
- Noise robustness in loud bars, DJ tempo changes and remixes are the main technical risks for ambient recognition. PeakNetFP-style research on time-stretching robustness ([arXiv 2506.21086](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.21086)) is relevant to DJ-heavy Cameroonian venues.

### Gaps
- I found no independent accuracy figures for any vendor in a Global South venue setting.
- I found no Kenyan venue-monitoring startup, despite searching.
