# Problems and Frontier Tech Opportunities in Cameroon's and Africa's Music and Creative Economy (2025–2026)

Research date: 2026-11-21. Scope: software only, with priority on 2025–2026 information. Some threads were thin (ticket fraud, Cameroon archives, Pex/Audible Magic 2026). These are flagged under Gaps.

## 1. Royalty collection and rights management in Cameroon (SONACAM, CMC/SOCAM history, payouts, metadata)

### Takeaway
Cameroon's music CMO, SONACAM, distributes tiny, opaque, flat-rate sums: 40M FCFA in total in Oct 2025, paid as a 27,000 FCFA "advance" per artist rather than in proportion to usage. That points to the root problem: there is no usage data (broadcast, bar or streaming logs) to distribute against. The first bar-related collection (a levy of 12 FCFA per beer crate, about 100M FCFA, announced July 2026) is a blanket proxy, not usage-based. The pain point a hackathon system could attack is "who played what, where, and how often."

### Cited Findings
**SONACAM distributions (amounts and recipients)**
- Oct 2025 distribution: the board chose a flat 27,000 FCFA per beneficiary, called an "advance on royalties", from a total envelope of 40 million FCFA. The communiqué was signed by DG Kabelok Ariane. Payouts began 7 Oct 2025 at Express Union Finance agencies; claimants needed an ID card and a mandate number. — [Mimi Mefo Info](https://mimimefoinfos.com/la-sonacam-indigne-les-artistes-camerounais-avec-27-000-fcfa-davance-de-droits-dauteur/); [Cameroun24](https://cameroun24.net/article/70951-SONACAM___debut_des_paiements_de_la_repartition_du.html); [SCN](https://sportsandculturenetwork.com/droits-dauteur-seulement-27-000-fcfa-par-artiste/)
- The board chair had promised distributions of 30,000–300,000 FCFA per rights holder. The son of makossa legend Eboa Lotin publicly reported receiving 2,000 FCFA, which caused outrage (Sept 2026 coverage). — [allAfrica](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202609230156.html); [Camer.be](https://www.camer.be/94843/1:6/cameroun-eboa-lotin-2-000-fcfa-de-droits-dauteur-la-colere.html)
- Distribution history, as compiled by a specialist blog: 2022, about 110M FCFA to about 1,500 artists; June 2023, about 80M FCFA to 2,040 artists (25,000–300,000 FCFA each); Oct 2025, 40M FCFA envelope. SONACAM operates under Law No. 2000/011 of 19 Dec 2000 and publishes no fixed distribution calendar. — [Muzisecur: Vivre de sa musique au Cameroun](https://www.muzisecur.fr/blog/vivre-de-sa-musique-cameroun-revenus-artiste/); [Muzisecur: calendrier SONACAM 2026](https://www.muzisecur.fr/blog/calendrier-paiement-sonacam-2026-repartition/) (secondary source; not official SONACAM data)
- Canal+ Afrique payments to Cameroonian CMOs reportedly rose from 70M to 150M FCFA per year (Cameroon Tribune, Dec 2023, as cited by the blog). — [Muzisecur](https://www.muzisecur.fr/blog/vivre-de-sa-musique-cameroun-revenus-artiste/); see also [Cameroon Tribune, "Droits d'auteur dans l'art musical : des notes d'espoir"](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/58312/fr.html/droits-dauteur-dans-lart-musical-notes-despoir)

**Bars and nightclubs: the new beer-crate levy (2026)**
- On 30 July 2026, SONACAM board chair Dr Ateh Ndong Francis announced recovery of 100 million FCFA in royalties collected at source via a levy of 12 FCFA per crate of beer sold. Cameroon Tribune calls it the first time such royalties were collected from the country's bars and lounges. — [Cameroon Tribune (EN)](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/79271/en.html/copyrights-windfall-sonacam-secures-landmark-100-million-fcfa); [Interspace Music](https://interspacemusic.com/blog/cameroon-collects-royalties-12-francs-crate-beer-last-payout-27000-francs/)
- The French version of Cameroon Tribune places collection at the wholesale level, through brewers' and distributors' logistics networks. The two versions differ slightly (bars and lounges vs wholesale). The money goes only to registered members. — [Cameroon Tribune (FR)](https://cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/79271/fr.html/details_2); [Interspace](https://interspacemusic.com/blog/cameroon-collects-royalties-12-francs-crate-beer-last-payout-27000-francs/)

**Governance history: CMC, SOCAM, SONACAM**
- Years of conflict between the Cameroon Music Corporation (CMC), part of SOCAM's membership, and a Cameroonian authors-composers society. The Prime Minister validated creating a new CMO for music ("category B") to end the conflict, and CMC leaders resisted, arguing the government could not interfere in private societies. — [Cameroun24, "Imbroglio autour de la gestion des droits d'auteurs musicaux"](https://cameroun24.net/article/40420-imbroglio-autour-de-la-gestion-des-droits-dauteurs-musicaux-au-cameroun.html)
- July 2022: the Minister of Arts and Culture (MINAC) warned against a "hold-up" on SONACAM after a former Socinada chair claimed provisional administrators had been appointed, based on a 30 May 2022 ruling of the Mfoundi High Court. MINAC and the SONACAM board appealed. — [Cameroon Tribune, "Droits d'auteur : la nouvelle affaire Sonacam"](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/49423/en.html/droits-dauteur-la-nouvelle-affaire-sonacam)

**Streaming and platform income for Cameroonian artists**
- Spotify has been available in Cameroon since Feb 2021, when it launched in 39 African countries. — [Africanews](https://www.africanews.com/2021/02/23/african-music-streamers-to-soon-access-spotify-as-it-launches-in-39-nations/); [Spotify Newsroom](https://newsroom.spotify.com/?p=9190)
- An estimated average of about €3.50 per 1,000 Spotify streams (about 2,300 FCFA, master share). Since 2024, Spotify does not pay for tracks under 1,000 annual streams. The YouTube Partner Program is not open to Cameroon (per Google's country list), so artists must rely on Content ID via distributors. — [Muzisecur](https://www.muzisecur.fr/blog/vivre-de-sa-musique-cameroun-revenus-artiste/) (blog estimate)
- Comparator, Nigeria: Spotify paid Nigerian artists about ₦60bn (about $43.8–43.9M) for 30.3bn streams in 2025, just under ₦2 per stream. That is about 0.39% of Spotify's more than $11bn global royalties. 58% went to independent artists or labels. Year-on-year growth was only about 3.45% (the oft-cited 140% is a three-year figure). TechCabal also reports that more local streams lower per-stream payouts, because Nigerian subscription prices are low. — [TechCabal (16 Mar 2026)](https://techcabal.com/2026/03/16/spotify-payouts-show-nigerian-artists-earned-about-₦2-per-stream-in-2025/); [TechCabal (23 Mar 2026)](https://techcabal.com/2026/03/23/local-streams-are-reducing-spotify-payouts-for-nigerian-artists/); [Music In Africa](https://www.musicinafrica.net/fr/node/385257)
- Public grants: the MINAC special allocation account (created 2001) funded 427 projects for about 222M FCFA in its May 2018 session, including 269 music projects at about 517,000 FCFA average. — [Muzisecur](https://www.muzisecur.fr/blog/vivre-de-sa-musique-cameroun-revenus-artiste/)

**African broadcast-monitoring pain (regional comparators)**
- Kenya: MCSK's inconsistent payouts are linked to irregularities in monitoring airplay, and broadcaster royalties remain "a huge problem." KECOBO's 2024 re-licensing triggered litigation, followed by six-month provisional licences for MCSK, KAMP and PAVRISK. — [Music In Africa](https://www.musicinafrica.net/magazine/broadcaster-royalties-kenya-still-huge-problem); [CIPIT Strathmore](https://cipit.strathmore.edu/?p=11485)
- Evidence from Nigeria, South Africa and Kenya shows that regulatory interventions often lead to conflict between regulators and CMOs. — [Afronomicslaw](https://www.afronomicslaw.org/print/pdf/node/2242)
- ARIPO has surveyed online operations of CMOs in its member states. This is a useful baseline for digital maturity, and Cameroon is not an ARIPO member. — [ARIPO CMO Survey Report](https://aripo.org/storage/copyright-publication/1724743652_The%20ARIPO%20Online%20Collective%20Management%20Organizations%20Survey%20Report.pdf)

**Metadata standards (building blocks)**
- DDEX publishes the standard XML message suites (ERN for releases, DSR for sales and usage reports, MWN for works notifications) used between labels, distributors, DSPs and CMOs. — [DDEX](https://ddex.net)

### Inferences
- SONACAM pays flat advances because it has no granular usage data. Any system that produces auditable, per-work usage logs (radio, TV, bars, events) directly attacks the "27,000 FCFA for everyone" failure.
- The 12 FCFA per crate levy creates a new pool of money with no distribution key. A sampled, privacy-preserving monitoring network in bars (cheap phones running audio fingerprinting) could provide the allocation key for that pot. This is a timely, local, 2026 story judges will recognise.
- Low trust after the CMC/SOCAM/SONACAM wars suggests a "transparency by design" angle: verifiable logs (hash-chained or Merkle-anchored), public dashboards, and member-checkable statements. A blockchain is unnecessary, since a transparent append-only log suffices.
- Mobile-money payout rails (MTN MoMo and Orange Money) could replace in-person Express Union collection with an ID card. That remains an inference; I found no source on SONACAM's plans.

### Gaps
- No official SONACAM annual report: total collections, cost ratio and member counts for 2024–2026 were not found.
- No reliable Cameroon-specific data on piracy losses, Boomplay or Audiomack payouts or market share, or radio/TV monitoring practice (whether SONACAM uses any fingerprinting vendor).
- Current status of the "category B" CMO dispute and the 2022 court case is unconfirmed.
- No Cameroon-specific ISRC/ISWC adoption figures or split-sheet dispute statistics were found.

## 2. Africa-wide market context (IFPI, CISAC)

### Takeaway
Africa is the fastest-growing but smallest region. Sub-Saharan recorded music reached about $120M in 2025 (+15.2%), and African authors' societies collected €90M in 2024 (+14.2%). South Africa takes about 78% of SSA recorded revenue, so francophone Central Africa, including Cameroon, is nearly invisible in the numbers.

### Cited Findings
- IFPI GMR 2025 (2024 data): SSA recorded revenue grew 22.6%, surpassing $100M for the first time (about $110M). — [Music In Africa](https://musicinafrica.net/node/358892); [MBW takeaways](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/11-quick-and-important-takeaways-from-ifpis-global-music-report-2025)
- IFPI GMR 2026 (2025 data): SSA up 15.2% to $120M. South Africa is 78.1% of the region's revenue, after 12.9% growth. Global recorded revenue rose 6% in 2025, and IFPI framed the industry as "embracing the future" on AI. — [Music In Africa](https://www.musicinafrica.net/node/385461); [Music Week](https://www.musicweek.com/public_html/index.php/labels/read/ifpi-global-recorded-music-revenue-up-6-in-2025-as-industry-embraces-the-future-on-ai/093789)
- CISAC GCR 2025 (2024 data): Africa collected €90M (+14.2%), more than 4 percentage points above any other region yet still the smallest region. It covers 24 countries and 26 member societies. Global creator royalties were €13.97bn. — [CISAC](https://www.cisac.org/cisac-global-collections-report-2025); [Downtown Music Africa](https://downtownmusic.africa/africa-collections-hit-e90m/); [GEMA](https://www.gema.de/en/w/cisac-global-collections-report-2025-creators-royalties-powered-by-digital-growth)
- The CISAC 2026 report (2025 data) puts global music collections up 12% to €14bn. — [Music Week](https://www.musicweek.com/publishing/read/cisac-music-collections-up-12-in-2025-to-eur14-billion/095400)

### Inferences
- Cameroon's 40M FCFA (about €61k) distribution is a rounding error against Africa's €90M. That gap is the "long-term industry impact" pitch: Cameroon is under-collecting relative to its cultural output.

### Gaps
- Whether SONACAM is a CISAC member in good standing, and Cameroon's line in the CISAC country tables, were not verified.

## 3. AI and music, 2025–2026: generation, lawsuits, licensing, attribution, detection, watermarking

### Takeaway
Since late 2025 the industry has pivoted from "sue" to "license plus attribute." UMG and WMG settled with Udio, WMG settled with Suno, STIM launched a collective AI licence that requires Sureel attribution, and WMG acquired Sureel in June 2026. Meanwhile AI floods the supply side: more than 50% of Deezer's daily uploads (about 90k per day) were AI-generated by mid-2026. Up to 70–85% of streams on fully AI tracks were fraudulent. No African CMO appears to have an AI licensing or attribution regime. Watermarks remain fragile, so fingerprinting plus attribution is the robust layer.

### Cited Findings
**Lawsuits and licensing deals**
- UMG settled with Udio in late Oct 2025 and will launch a new licensed creation and streaming platform in 2026. WMG settled with Udio about a week later, with a licensing deal for a 2026 service. — [Hollywood Reporter](https://hollywoodreporter.com/music/music-industry-news/universal-music-group-announces-settlement-with-udio-1236414023); [MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/warner-music-settles-udio-lawsuit-strikes-licensing-deal-with-ai-platform)
- WMG settled its part of the $500M suit against Suno (Nov 2025) and signed a licence. Suno acquired Songkick from WMG, and terms were undisclosed. As of Dec 2025, UMG and Sony were still litigating against Suno, and Sony against Udio. — [MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/warner-music-group-settles-with-suno/); [Billboard](https://billboard.com/pro/suno-warner-music-sign-ai-licensing-deal-settle-lawsuit/); [Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2025/12/18/sony-music-udio-suno-lawsuit-updates/)

**Attribution and training-data royalties**
- Sept 2025: Sweden's STIM launched what it called the world's first collective AI licence for music, and AI developers must integrate Sureel's attribution tech into training. Sureel's patent describes learning "static attribution vectors" that estimate each training example's percentage of influence on an output. — [Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2025/09/26/stim-taps-sureel-ai-attribution-tracking/); [DeepLearning.AI The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/swedens-stim-built-an-ecosystem-for-training-ai-models-on-copyrighted-music-and-compensating-original-artists)
- June 2026: WMG acquired Sureel. Its "AI DNA" breaks songs into components to trace how AI models use them. Sureel also partnered with distributor Symphonic so indie artists can track AI use of their music. IMPEL and IMPF ran a sandbox with Sureel for independent publishers. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/10/warner-music-acquires-ai-attribution-startup-sureel-ai); [MBW](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/warner-music-group-acquires-sureel-ai-the-attribution-startup-that-traces-how-ai-models-use-artists-work/); [MBW on Symphonic](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/wmgs-sureel-teams-up-with-symphonic-to-let-distro-companys-artists-track-ai-use-of-their-music/); [Music Week](https://www.musicweek.com/public_html/index.php/publishing/read/impel-and-impf-collaborate-on-sandbox-project-with-sureel-ai/094618); [Music In Africa (FR)](https://www.musicinafrica.net/fr/node/393227)
- Musical AI (formerly SOMMS AI) provides attribution and rights-compliance infrastructure for generative music and raised $4.5M on 13 Jan 2026. Water & Music contrasts model-level influence tracking (Musical AI, Sureel, Soundverse) with simple pro-rata splits by dataset share. — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/compare/sommsai-vs-sureelai); [Water & Music](https://www.waterandmusic.com/music-ai-attribution)
- WIPO Magazine covers models for paying artists for AI-generated songs. — [WIPO Magazine](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/wipo-magazine/articles/royalties-in-the-age-of-ai-paying-artists-for-ai-generated-songs-73739)

**AI detection and rights-protection vendors**
- Vermillio (TraceID) raised a $16M Series A led by Sony Music and DNS Capital (Feb–Mar 2025). TraceID tracks online use of IP and name/image/likeness (NIL), issues takedowns and manages licensing payments, and uses stem-level detection (vocals, melodic phrasing, lyrics) to flag AI segments. — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/03/03/vermillio-ai-rights-licensing-sony-music); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250228026074/en/Vermillio-Completes-$16-Million-Series-A-Funding-Round-Led-by-Sony-Music-and-DNS-Capital); [Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2025/03/03/vermillio-series-a/)
- Pex, Audible Magic, SoundCloud and Rightsify are grouped as expanding detection, moderation and attribution features. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/ai-detection-could-reinvent-music-licensing-open-revenue-streams)

**AI content flood and streaming fraud (Deezer data)**
- Deezer: AI uploads were about 10k per day in Jan 2025, when it launched its detector; about 75k per day (44%) in Apr 2026; and more than 50% of daily deliveries (about 90k per day) in June 2026. AI tracks are 1–3% of streams, yet up to 85% of streams on fully AI tracks were fraudulent in 2025 (70% cited in Apr 2026) and were demonetised. Fraud overall was 8% of all streams in 2025. — [Deezer Newsroom, Jul 2026](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/07/ai-music-exceeds-50-percent-daily-uploads-deezer/); [Deezer Newsroom, Apr 2026](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/04/ai-generated-tracks-represent-44-of-new-uploaded-music/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/21/music-streamer-deezer-says-more-than-50-of-daily-uploads-are-ai-generated/) (self-reported by Deezer)

**Audio watermarking robustness (research)**
- AudioMarkBench (NeurIPS 2024 D&B) tested AudioSeal, Timbre and WavMark against 15 perturbations. They resist no-box and black-box forgery but are vulnerable to white-box forgery. EnCodec re-encoding can remove watermarks, and the authors found robustness gaps across sex and language groups. — [arXiv 2406.06979](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06979v2)
- "SoK: How Robust is Audio Watermarking in Generative AI models?" (2025) evaluated 22 schemes against 22 removal attacks, and none was robust to physical-level distortion (for example, playback through a speaker and re-recording). — [arXiv 2503.19176](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19176v2)
- A 2025 benchmark compares AudioSeal, SilentCipher, Timbre and WavMark under mixing, noise, reverb, compression and EQ, plus distortion-aware training. — [arXiv 2505.19663](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.19663v1)

**Open-source building blocks (primary repos)**
- AudioSeal (Meta): localized, sample-level audio watermarking with a detector. — [GitHub facebookresearch/audioseal](https://github.com/facebookresearch/audioseal)
- Demucs (Meta): music source and stem separation (vocals, drums, bass, other). — [GitHub facebookresearch/demucs](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs)
- Chromaprint/AcoustID: open audio fingerprinting library and database. — [GitHub acoustid/chromaprint](https://github.com/acoustid/chromaprint)
- Panako: acoustic fingerprinting robust to pitch shift and time stretch. — [GitHub JorenSix/Panako](https://github.com/JorenSix/Panako)
- audfprint: landmark-based (Shazam-style) fingerprinting in Python. — [GitHub dpwe/audfprint](https://github.com/dpwe/audfprint)
- C2PA: an open standard for content provenance manifests, which covers audio formats. — [C2PA](https://c2pa.org)

### Inferences
- The robustness research means a Shazam-style landmark fingerprint (audfprint or Panako) is the realistic way to detect music played in a noisy Cameroonian bar or on FM radio re-recorded by a phone mic. Watermarks are useful only as a provenance signal on the digital path.
- An "African STIM" angle: a collective AI licence and attribution registry for Cameroonian repertoire. Makossa and bikutsi are absent from global training-data deals, so no one is attributing them, and generative models can already imitate them. A prototype could combine embedding similarity (CLAP or MERT-style music embeddings) with a fingerprint and stem pipeline (Demucs) to produce influence or similarity scores against a local catalogue. This is an inference; Cameroon-specific voice-cloning cases were not found.
- 48h feasibility: audfprint/Chromaprint indexing of a few hundred tracks with phone-mic queries is very feasible. Demucs inference on a GPU laptop is feasible. AudioSeal embed and detect is feasible. True training-data attribution like Sureel's is not feasible in 48h, but a retrieval-based "nearest influences" proxy can be demonstrated.

### Gaps
- No SynthID-for-audio robustness benchmark was found.
- No 2026 confirmation of UMG/Sony vs Suno status (latest found: Dec 2025, still litigating).
- No reporting found on ProRata's music-specific mechanics, or on 2025–2026 launches by BMAT, Pex or Audible Magic in Africa.
- No documented cases of AI voice clones of Cameroonian artists were found in this pass.

## 4. Live events: ticketing fraud, cashless payments, crowd safety

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin. I found no Cameroon- or Nigeria-specific data on fake tickets sold via mobile money. Global patterns (fake sites, social-media resale, screenshots of static QR codes) plus mobile money's irreversibility suggest rotating, signed tickets that verify offline are a credible but unproven pain point.

### Cited Findings
- CIMFEST 2025 (4th edition): community outreach at Bolifamba, Mile 16 (15 Nov); an exhibition at Molyko Omnisport Stadium, Buea, with more than 170 vendors (19–22 Nov); awards night on 22 Nov; nightly concerts. The 48h hackathon had 75 young developers from across Cameroon working on entertainment-industry problems, alongside a tech symposium on AI for the industry. Winners were not named in the article. — [Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/74231/en.html/cameroon-international-music-festival-entertainment-blends-with-business-in)
- Ticket price range in Cameroon: Niska's concert at the Palais polyvalent des sports, Yaoundé (21 Dec 2024), ran from 10,000 FCFA (standard) to 100,000 FCFA (VVIP). — [Muzisecur](https://www.muzisecur.fr/blog/vivre-de-sa-musique-cameroun-revenus-artiste/)
- Nigeria, Dec 2025: fans who had paid were denied entry to BNXN's Landmark Event Centre concert, and the artist and organisers traded blame. This was an access-control and oversell failure, not documented fraud. — [Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/?p=2749455)
- Global pattern: fraudulent websites account for 38% of ticket-scam reports to the BBB and Facebook for 28%. Rotating-barcode mobile tickets make screenshots worthless. — [Bitdefender](https://www.bitdefender.com/blog/hotforsecurity/fake-ticket-scams/); [Surfshark research](https://surfshark.com/research/chart/concert-ticket-fraud)

### Inferences
- A good hackathon fit is offline-verifiable signed tickets (Ed25519-signed, time-rotating TOTP-style QR codes) checked at the gate without connectivity, plus anti-resale rules and mobile-money settlement. Connectivity at Molyko stadium is likely spotty, but that is unverified.
- Cashless festival wallets with MoMo/Orange Money top-up, linked to the 170+ vendors, could produce vendor sales data. That data could feed artist and festival analytics.

### Gaps
- No Cameroon data on ticket fraud volumes, MoMo festival payments, or crowd-safety incidents.
- MTN MoMo and Orange Money API availability for merchant payments in Cameroon was not researched here; other researchers may cover it.

## 5. Data and financing: revenue-based financing, catalogue valuation, fan data, direct-to-fan

### Takeaway
Revenue streams are so small and undocumented in Cameroon (flat CMO advances, no YouTube Partner Program, low Spotify per-stream rates) that financing is blocked by a data problem. A credible, verifiable "royalty data ledger" is the prerequisite for any revenue-based financing (RBF) or catalogue-valuation product.

### Cited Findings
- No YouTube Partner Program in Cameroon, Spotify pays nothing for tracks under 1,000 streams per year, and there is no public scale of artist live fees in Cameroon. — [Muzisecur](https://www.muzisecur.fr/blog/vivre-de-sa-musique-cameroun-revenus-artiste/)
- Nigerian artists: 58% of 2025 Spotify royalties went to independents, so there is a large unsigned segment that needs non-label financing. — [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2026/03/16/spotify-payouts-show-nigerian-artists-earned-about-₦2-per-stream-in-2025/)
- Brand sync example: Orange Money's 2024 campaign used a reworked version of Lydol's "Bango Bango"; the fee was undisclosed. — [Muzisecur](https://www.muzisecur.fr/blog/vivre-de-sa-musique-cameroun-revenus-artiste/)

### Inferences
- A system that aggregates verified usage (fingerprint detections, DSP reports via DDEX DSR, MoMo ticket sales) into a per-artist cash-flow history could underwrite micro-advances. That is a technically deep but explainable design story.

### Gaps
- No African RBF-for-artists provider data (for example, African players comparable to beatBread or Duetti) was found in this pass. Fan-data ownership products for Africa were not researched.

## 6. Cultural preservation: endangered Cameroonian genres and languages

### Takeaway
Important Cameroonian recordings sit in European archives (CREM-CNRS Paris, Berlin Phonogramm-Archiv, Hildesheim), often listed as "metadata only". I found no national Cameroonian digitisation programme. Repatriation, searchable indexing and community access are open problems.

### Cited Findings
- CREM-CNRS holds bikutsi field recordings by Susanne Fürniss with Kisito Essélé Essélé (Eton area, 2009 mission, Baka and Beti Eton populations) and the 1977 LP "Musique traditionnelle du Cameroun vol. 1: chants des Bamum" (metadata only). — [CREM item](https://archives.crem-cnrs.fr/archives/items/CNRSMH_I_2016_021_004_025); [CREM collection](https://archives.crem-cnrs.fr/archives/collections/CNRSMH_E_1979_007_001/)
- Wax-cylinder recordings from South Cameroon (1907–1909) were copied and digitised by the Berlin Phonogramm-Archiv and returned to villages. Fürniss discusses the ethics of decontextualised sound. — [Fürniss 2016, HAL](https://halshs.archives-ouvertes.fr/halshs-02401385v1/file/Fu%CC%88rniss%202016%20Convergences.pdf)
- The Hildesheim Center for World Music holds the Archiv Arc Musica, compiled by Joachim Oelsner, who worked in Yaoundé. — [Uni Hildesheim](https://www.uni-hildesheim.de/en/center-for-world-music/archive-/-collections/archive-arc-music/)
- Makossa originates on Cameroon's coast and is typical of the Sawa people; bikutsi is another major popular rhythm. — [Music In Africa](https://musicinafrica.net/magazine/traditional-music-cameroon/)

### Inferences
- A "living archive" system fits a 48h build: ingest field recordings, use Demucs to separate stems, use music-tagging embeddings to classify rhythm families (makossa, bikutsi, assiko, mangambeu), add speech recognition and language ID on lyrics where models exist, and attach C2PA provenance plus community consent and licensing metadata. Cameroon has about 250 languages, though that figure was not sourced in this pass, and ASR support for most is absent.
- The same fingerprint and embedding index could serve preservation and rights (sections 1 and 3), giving one coherent platform story.

### Gaps
- No sources were found on assiko, bend skin or mangambeu archiving, on a Cameroonian national sound archive, or on ASR and language-ID coverage of Cameroonian languages (Duala, Ewondo, Bulu, Medumba and others).

## 7. Global music-tech landscape and what's missing for Africa

### Takeaway
Global players (Sureel/WMG, Musical AI, Vermillio/Sony, Deezer's detector, Pex, Audible Magic, BMAT) serve major labels and Western CMOs. I found no evidence of any of them operating attribution, AI detection or broadcast monitoring for Cameroonian or Central African repertoire. That is the whitespace.

### Cited Findings
- Attribution: Sureel (acquired by WMG in 2026; STIM's attribution provider; Symphonic partnership), Musical AI ($4.5M raised Jan 2026). — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/10/warner-music-acquires-ai-attribution-startup-sureel-ai); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/compare/sommsai-vs-sureelai)
- Rights protection: Vermillio TraceID (Sony-backed). — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/03/03/vermillio-ai-rights-licensing-sony-music)
- Detection at DSP level: Deezer's AI detector, launched Jan 2025. — [Deezer Newsroom](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/04/ai-generated-tracks-represent-44-of-new-uploaded-music/)
- Africa's CMO weakness is structural: monitoring irregularities (Kenya), CMO–regulator conflicts (Nigeria, SA, Kenya), and flat payouts (Cameroon). — [Music In Africa](https://www.musicinafrica.net/magazine/broadcaster-royalties-kenya-still-huge-problem); [Afronomicslaw](https://www.afronomicslaw.org/print/pdf/node/2242); [Mimi Mefo Info](https://mimimefoinfos.com/la-sonacam-indigne-les-artistes-camerounais-avec-27-000-fcfa-davance-de-droits-dauteur/)

### Inferences: candidate hackathon system concepts (ranked by fit with innovation, feasibility and impact)
1. **"SONACAM Ledger" / Open Usage Network**: phone-based passive listening nodes in bars and radio-capture workers, using landmark fingerprinting (audfprint or Panako) against a catalogue keyed by ISRC/ISWC. A tamper-evident hash-chained log, plus a distribution engine that allocates the 12 FCFA/crate pot and broadcast fees by measured plays, with MoMo payout simulation and a public transparency dashboard. Deep system design: edge capture, noise-robust matching, sampling statistics for representativeness, anti-gaming (fake play injection), privacy (fingerprints only, no raw audio). This is highly feasible in 48h with a demo catalogue.
2. **AI provenance and attribution layer for African repertoire**: AudioSeal and C2PA on release, a fingerprint plus embedding-similarity "influence report" for AI-generated tracks, an AI-content flag (in the spirit of Deezer's), and a collective AI-licence registry modelled on STIM's. It is innovative; feasibility is medium.
3. **Living Archive**: stem separation, rhythm-family classification and provenance with consent metadata for makossa, bikutsi and others. High cultural impact; medium feasibility.
4. **Offline-verifiable festival tickets plus cashless MoMo wallet**: lower novelty and thin evidence, but immediately usable at CIMFEST.

- No data on Boomplay or Audiomack per-stream rates or Cameroon user numbers.
- Past CIMFEST hackathon winners and judging rubric were not found online; only the 2025 hackathon format was confirmed.
