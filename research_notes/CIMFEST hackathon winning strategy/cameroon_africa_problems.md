# High-Value, Underexplored Problems in Cameroon and Africa for Software

Scope note: Research date 2026-11-21. About 20 searches and fetches. Most Cameroon-specific figures come from Cameroonian press (camer.be, AllAfrica FR, Cameroon Tribune) and UN agency pages. Several numbers are single-source or come from search summaries rather than full documents, and they are flagged where that applies. The "internet is expensive or slow" and "build an offline app" framings are deliberately excluded.

## 1. Trust and verification (land titles, fake diplomas, counterfeit drugs, identity, informal credit, mobile money fraud)

### Takeaway
Cameroon has a documented "trust deficit" problem: fraudulent documents, fake land titles and falsified medicines. Each has hard numbers and named institutions, but verification today is manual, siloed or limited to one institution. A cross-institution verification layer would be a genuine technical novelty, for example signed credentials plus a public verification API, a tamper-evident land-transaction log, or pharmacy batch authentication. Mobile money fraud is huge but poorly quantified in public data, which is itself an opening for a data-driven product.

### Cited Findings
**Fake diplomas and documents**
- In October 2024, CONAC (National Anti-Corruption Commission) launched a national campaign against fake diplomas used by candidates for public-service jobs. — [AllAfrica/Camer.be, July 2026](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202607130411.html)
- 1,312 fake diplomas were detected at the final stage of defence-forces recruitment. — [AllAfrica/Camer.be, July 2026](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202607130411.html)
- In September 2025, MINDEF struck off more than 1,000 recruitment candidates after mass fake-diploma fraud. — [AllAfrica, Sept 2025](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202509250582.html)
- 2025 baccalauréat: 255 people were sanctioned for fraud (208 candidates and 47 teachers). — [AllAfrica/Camer.be](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202607130411.html)
- The foreign-diploma equivalence commission reviewed 2,883 files and found 17 fake certificates. — [AllAfrica/Camer.be](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202607130411.html)
- In June 2026, a counterfeiting market for stamps, diplomas and fiscal stamps was dismantled in Efoulan, Yaoundé. — [AllAfrica, June 2026](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202606240180.html)
- Minister Henri Eyebé Ayissi said "the era of titles without verification is over." This relates to a diploma-verification platform for public-service recruitment. — [AllAfrica, June 2026](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202606220784.html)
- A Belgian COI report (Oct 2024) documents corruption and widespread document fraud in Cameroon. — [ecoi.net COI Focus](https://www.ecoi.net/en/file/local/2116702/coi_focus_cameroun._corruption_et_fraude_documentaire_20241014.pdf)
- UNVERIFIED: a search summary attributed to a June 2026 article said that in 2024, 974 fake diplomas were found among 4,981 public-service files. The fetched article did not contain this figure.

Most land is "national land" held under customary law. — [Land Portal / Tetra Tech](https://landportal.org/node/217931); [africa.com](https://africa.com/feature/cameroon-land-and-property-rights)

It says a land title takes about 2 years to obtain and can cost up to 15% of the land's value. The underlying data is not shown, so treat the figures as unverified. — [AllAfrica, July 2026](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202607010658.html)
- In November 2025, MINDCAF suspended 21 cadastre agents for falsifying land maps, withholding files, overbilling and repeated fraud. In June 2026, files were still reported as disappearing and informal payments were still being demanded. — [AllAfrica, June 2026](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202606040422.html)
- January 2026: COMICODI denounced influence-peddling around fake land titles. — [camer.be](https://www.camer.be/92000/11:1/cameroun-trafics-dinfluence-sur-les-faux-titres-fonciers-la-comicodi-interpelle-le-ministre-des-domaines-cameroon.html)
- May 2026: land predation and influence-peddling threats were reported in Douala and Yaoundé. — [AllAfrica, May 2026](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202605130384.html)
- Courts and other conflict-resolution institutions are "highly ineffective and overwhelmed." — [Land Portal](https://landportal.org/node/217931)

**Counterfeit and substandard medicines**
- WHO: 1 in 10 medical products in low- and middle-income countries is substandard or falsified. In sub-Saharan Africa, falsified or substandard antibiotics for child pneumonia are linked to about 72,000 to 169,000 child deaths a year, and bad antimalarials to 31,000 to 116,000 deaths a year. — [WHO DG remarks, Aug 2022](https://www.who.int/director-general/speeches/detail/who-director-general-s-opening-remarks-at-the-fighting-substandard-and-falsified-medicines-in-africa---23-august-2022)
- UNODC (2023): up to 267,000 deaths a year are linked to falsified or substandard antimalarials. Trafficked medical products kill almost 500,000 sub-Saharan Africans a year. — [UN News, Feb 2023](https://news.un.org/en/story/2023/02/1133062)
- Cameroon: a study of anti-infective tablets in three cities found 7.9% substandard or falsified overall, 26.7% in informal outlets and 2.6% in formal ones. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9896317)
- A critical review of Cameroon studies found a pooled substandard or falsified prevalence of 26.9%. It notes the problem is understudied. — [AJTMH review (ORBi)](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/261031/1/%5b14761645%20-%20The%20American%20Journal%20of%20Tropical%20Medicine%20and%20Hygiene%5d%20Poor-Quality%20Medicines%20in%20Cameroon_%20A%20Critical%20Review.pdf)

**Mobile money fraud and SIM identity**
- GSMA: more than $2 trillion moved through mobile money globally in 2025. Most new registered and active accounts came from sub-Saharan Africa. GSMA lists consumer protection and fraud controls as a priority. — [GSMA press release](https://www.gsma.com/newsroom/press-release/mobile-money-accounted-for-2-trillion-in-transactions-in-2025-doubling-since-2021-as-active-accounts-continue-to-grow/)
- GSMA Mobile Economy Africa 2025 has a dedicated section, "Tackling mobile fraud and the scam economy". The figures were not retrieved. — [GSMA ME Africa 2025 PDF](https://www.gsma.com/solutions-and-impact/connectivity-for-good/mobile-economy/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/GSMA_AFRICA_ME2025_R_Web-3.pdf)
- A GSMA Africa Fraud and Security Group presentation to ITU (Jan 2026) says identity fraud (impersonation) is the most prevalent mobile money fraud type, at 90%. Customer complaints are the top detection channel, at 96%. Only a fragment was retrieved. — [ITU webinar PDF](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-T/webinars/dfs/20260127/Documents/Sobreira.pdf)
- Common scams include fake "mistaken transfer" refund requests and USSD scams. — [Thales](https://www.thalesgroup.com/en/news-centre/insights/enterprise/access-engagement-where-next-mobile-money)
- MTN Cameroon ran an anti-fraud MoMo education campaign in September 2025. It uses the fraud.mtn.cm reporting page and automated detection. — [AllAfrica, Sept 2025](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202509300387.html)
- Fraud types in Cameroon include social engineering, SIM swaps, smishing, agent-driven fraud and PIN sharing. Operators must comply with ANTIC requirements, including incident reporting and pen-testing. The source's dating is inconsistent. — [Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/cameroon-fintech-platforms-navigating-mobile-money-fraud-or-unauthorized-transactions/)
- April 2024: authorities ordered MTN and Orange to reinforce SIM identification within 60 days. Ecofin reported that terrorists netted ransoms through mobile money (headline figure "524,100"; the currency was not visible in the snippet). — [Ecofin](https://ecofinagency.com/telecom/1204-45389-cameroon-cracks-down-on-unregistered-sim-cards-as-terrorists-net-524-100-in-ransoms-via-mobile-money)
- April 2026: Yaoundé cases of mobile money used for real-time extortion during kidnappings ("séquestration"). Commentators call for a "forced transaction" alert. — [AllAfrica, April 2026](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202604080164.html)
- Regional analogue: SIM-swap fraud is worsening in the DRC because of weak enforcement. — [ENACT, Dec 2025](https://enactafrica.org/enact-observer/weak-enforcement-measures-exacerbate-sim-swapping-in-the-drc)

**Informal credit and reputation**
- Bank account ownership in Cameroon is about 12.2%, against a 29% sub-Saharan Africa average. The figure is older and its year is unclear. Credit databases are fragmented and national, and informal micro-SMEs lack collateral and credit-scoring tools. — [CAMEPI](https://camepi.org/?p=3355)
- Global Findex 2025 microdata for Cameroon (survey year 2024) exists, but only unweighted counts were retrieved. — [World Bank Microdata](https://microdata.worldbank.org/catalog/7880)

### Inferences
- **Cross-institution credential verification.** Each body currently verifies its own documents: MINESUP, OBC, MINFOPRA, MINDEF and the equivalence commission. A common verifiable-credential standard, such as W3C VC with QR codes and an issuer registry, is a real systems problem, not a CRUD app. It maps globally to Europass, the EU EBSI and India's DigiLocker.
- **Land.** With under 2% of land registered historically and a large share of court cases tied to land, a tamper-evident, append-only transaction log would hit the core fraud vector: multiple sales and forged maps. Such a log could use Merkle-anchored records, geo-referenced parcel claims and notary and community witnessing. Global analogues include Georgia's and Ghana's land-registry blockchain pilots, Rwanda's LTR, and India's SVAMITVA drone mapping.
- **Mobile money.** Fraud is impersonation-dominated and complaint-detected (GSMA: 90% identity fraud, 96% detection by complaint). This points to an opening for real-time behavioural anomaly or "duress" detection and a shared scam-number intelligence network across MTN and Orange. Global analogues include the UK's Confirmation of Payee, Brazil's PIX MED, and India's UPI fraud registries.
- **Medicines.** The informal-outlet substandard rate (26.7%) is about 10 times the formal one (2.6%). Batch authentication at the point of sale, using serialization lookup, GS1 codes and photo-based packaging anomaly detection, is a strong angle. Existing attempts such as mPedigree and Sproxil (SMS scratch codes) exist in Ghana and Nigeria. No Cameroon deployment was found.

### Gaps
- No official 2024–2026 Cameroon statistics on mobile money fraud volume or value (from ANTIC, BEAC, COBAC, MTN or Orange) were found.
- No Cameroon-specific SIM-swap counts were found.
- No current national land-registration percentage was found.
- The 974/4,981 fake-diploma figure is unconfirmed.
- No Cameroon credit bureau (BEAC CIP) coverage figure was found.
- No evidence was found of Sproxil or mPedigree-style drug authentication operating in Cameroon. Absence was not confirmed.

## 2. Language and knowledge access (250+ languages, bilingual administration, Pidgin, Camfranglais)

### Takeaway
Cameroon has about 273 living indigenous languages. Cameroonian Pidgin is spoken in some form by roughly half the population, yet it has almost no NLP resources. Administration runs only in French and English. Language tooling for Pidgin and major national languages, such as speech-to-text or voice Q&A over legal, administrative and health information, is underexplored and globally relevant to low-resource AI.

### Cited Findings
- Ethnologue lists 273 living indigenous languages in Cameroon plus 9 extinct ones. Other sources say "at least 250" or more. — [Ethnologue](https://Ethnologue.com/country/CM); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Cameroon)
- Cameroonian Pidgin English has about 12 million speakers (2017). About 5% speak it natively, and an estimated 50% of the population speaks it in some form. — [Wikipedia: Cameroonian Pidgin](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/ISO_639:wes)
- AfricaNLP 2025 paper on Medumba (Grassfields Bantu) documents "extremely low-resource" dataset collection challenges. The authors built a French–Medumba parallel corpus. — [ACL Anthology, AfricaNLP 2025](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-dnd/2025.africanlp-1.18.pdf)
- Translators without Borders: there is little literature, radio or TV in Cameroon's indigenous languages. — [TWB language data for Cameroon](https://translatorswithoutborders.org/language-data-for-cameroon)

### Inferences
- Bilingual administration combined with the Anglophone crisis means legal and administrative documents (land, civil status, court procedures) are often unreadable for people literate only in the other official language, or not literate at all.
- A voice-first assistant would close that gap. It could run on Pidgin, French and English plus a few national languages such as Ewondo, Fulfulde, Duala and Bamileke languages, and use retrieval-augmented generation over official texts. Its novel parts are code-switching ASR (Camfranglais) and grounding in official sources.
- Global analogues include India's Bhashini, Masakhane, Ghana's Khaya AI and Lelapa AI's Vulavula in South Africa.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 survey was found on literacy by official language or on how many citizens cannot read administrative French or English.
- No Pidgin ASR or MT benchmark specific to Cameroon was found. Nigerian Pidgin has some resources, which could be a transfer source.
- No data was found on Camfranglais speaker counts.

## 3. Health (maternal health, malaria, record fragmentation, pharmacy supply, misinformation)

### Takeaway
Maternal mortality is still high and recent aid cuts threaten progress. DHIS2 handles aggregate reporting, but patient-level records are fragmented, with no national unique health identifier and low interoperability. Combined with counterfeit drugs (Section 1), this points to patient-held or interoperable records and supply-chain integrity as the high-value areas.

### Cited Findings
- UNICEF Cameroon (April 2025) cites a maternal mortality ratio of 258 per 100,000 live births for 2023 and a national target of 140 by 2030. Aid cuts threaten progress. Context in the release is ambiguous about whether 258 is Cameroon-specific. — [UNICEF Cameroon](https://www.unicef.org/cameroon/press-releases/aid-cuts-threaten-fragile-progress-ending-maternal-deaths-un-agencies-warn)
- Our World in Data gives 438 per 100,000 for 2020. UNICEF reports a drop from 782 to 406 between 2011 and 2018. Methodologies conflict. — [OWID](https://ourworldindata.org/profile/health/cameroon); [UNICEF Health](https://www.unicef.org/cameroon/what-we-do/health-hiv)
- Malaria: Yaoundé hosted the March 2024 ministerial conference of the 11 highest-burden countries, which produced the Yaoundé Declaration. — [WHO](https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/yaounde-declaration-for-accelerated-malaria-mortality-reduction-in-africa)
- A WHO 2024 Cameroon malaria profile exists, but numeric totals were not extracted. — [WHO malaria profile CMR 2024](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/country-profiles/malaria/malaria-2024-cmr.pdf)
- DHIS2 is the national HMIS backbone but is mainly aggregate. Individual records sit in separate, non-integrated subsystems. — [MINSANTE DHIS2 operational plan](https://files.aho.afro.who.int/afahobckpcontainer/production/files/CIS_Cameroon_DHIS2_operational_Plan_2022_to_2024.pdf); [Research Square](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-10135250)
- A 2025 Frontiers study finds "critical challenges" in digital-health governance, policy support and interoperability in Cameroon. — [Frontiers in Pharmacology 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2025.1490778/full)
- Unique identifiers, patient tracking and data security are only partially funded, with "no robust national framework" (2025). — [CQUIN/ICAP presentation](https://cquin.icap.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Session-9b-ME-Parallel-CAMEROON-for-website.pdf)
- ICD-11 is piloted in only 33 facilities. — [Research Square](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-10135250)
- openIMIS is being used for Cameroon's universal health coverage (UHC/CSU) rollout. — [BMZ Health](https://health.bmz.de/stories/openimis-powers-cameroons-long-term-vision-for-universal-health-coverage/)
- MINSANTE has a national digital-health strategic plan. — [MINSANTE PSNSN](https://www.minsante.cm/site/sites/default/files/EN_DOCUMENT_PLAN%20STRATEGIQUE%20NATIONAL%20DE%20SANTE%20NUMERIQUE_R%C3%A9duit.pdf)
- Street-drug market: one reported industry claim says up to 70% of drugs in Yaoundé are traded on the black market. It is unverified, and the source itself says there are no precise figures. — via [NPR/WHO 2017 coverage](https://www.npr.org/sections/goatsandsoda/2017/11/29/567229552/bad-drugs-are-a-major-globalproblem-who-reports)

### Inferences
- A patient-held, portable, consented health record bridges facilities that lack a national ID. It could be FHIR-based, use a QR or smart card, and support maternal care and antenatal visits. It fits the openIMIS and UHC rollout and the identifier gap.
- Global analogues include India's ABHA/ABDM and Estonia's e-Health.
- Pairing such a record with drug-batch verification gives a "trusted care" story.

### Gaps
- No authoritative 2024 malaria death and case totals for Cameroon were retrieved from the World Malaria Report.
- No recent Cameroon health-misinformation study was found.
- No data was found on stockout rates of essential medicines.

## 4. Economy (informal sector, tontines, cross-border trade, EUDR cocoa and coffee traceability)

### Takeaway
About 87% of Cameroonian jobs are informal, and credit and reputation infrastructure is weak. Cross-border trade with Nigeria and CEMAC is mostly informal and runs on hawala-like settlement, while PAPSS uptake is poor. The sharpest, deadline-driven opportunity is EUDR. Large operators must comply by 30 December 2026 and micro or small ones by 30 June 2027. Cameroon claims 99% traceability, but compliance costs are about 200 CFA/kg, the data is fragmented, and mapping was done mostly by large exporters. That leaves smallholders and cooperatives exposed.

### Cited Findings
**Informal sector and tontines**
- INS EESI3 (2021 survey, published 2023) breaks down employment as follows:

  | Sector | Share of employment |
  |---|---|
  | Informal, total | 86.6% |
  | Informal non-agricultural | 52.0% |
  | Informal agricultural | 34.7% |
  | Public sector | 8.2% |
  | Formal private | 5.1% |

  Only 2.1% of informal workers have a written contract. — [INS EESI3 Phase 1](https://ins-cameroun.cm/en/statistique/eesi3-phase-1-employment-survey-main-report/); [INS EESI3 Phase 2](https://ins-cameroun.cm/en/statistique/eesi3-phase-2-survey-on-the-informal-sector-main-report/)
- Informal workers earn an average of CFA 83,409 a month. — [Ecofin, Nov 2023](https://ecofinagency.com/public-management/0611-45017-cameroons-informal-workers-earn-an-average-of-cfa83-409-per-month-ins)
- An OECD social-protection financing report for Cameroon covers formalisation. One source says tontines account for 12.9% of financing, but the population and measure are unclear. — [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/fr/publications/financement-de-la-protection-sociale-au-cameroun_526eff6e-fr/full-report/developper-l-economie-formelle_ea6acf93.html)

**Cross-border trade**
- Official non-oil trade between Cameroon and Nigeria is "extremely small", meaning most of it is informal. — [World Bank](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/bitstreams/ad65424e-77f4-55ac-9fa0-d15c957faafc/download)
- Roadblocks occur about every 15 km with endemic informal payments (2013 data). — [ITC](https://intracen.org/news-and-events/news/unblocking-border-gridlock)
- On the Cameroon–Chad corridor, insecurity and harassment by police, gendarmerie and customs cripple trade. — [ISS](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/insecurity-is-crippling-economies-on-the-cameroon-chad-trade-corridor)
- PAPSS: Nigerian settlements fell 53% in H1 2025, to 3,246 transactions. SME awareness was below 20%, and traders rely on hawala. This is an opinion piece. — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/opinion/article/northern-nigerias-traders-already-have-a-cross-border-settlement-system-it-is-not-afcfta/)
- September 2026: Nigeria and Cameroon launched a border-modernisation project at the Mfum–Ekok one-stop border post (digital customs, joint risk management, AfCFTA). — [Nairametrics, Sept 2026](https://nairametrics.com/2026/09/10/nigeria-cameroon-launch-border-modernisation-project-to-ease-trade-barriers/)

**EUDR and cocoa/coffee**
- Regulation (EU) 2025/2650 (December 2025) postponed EUDR again. Large operators must comply from 30 December 2026, and micro or small enterprises and natural persons from 30 June 2027. A simplification review was due by 30 April 2026. — [EU Access2Markets](https://trade.ec.europa.eu/access-to-markets/en/news/delay-until-december-2026-and-other-developments-implementation-eudr-regulation); [EY](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2026-0237-eu-deforestation-regulation-application-postponed-to-30-december-2026); [Mongabay](https://news.mongabay.com/short-article/eudr-antideforestation-law-officially-delayed-for-second-year-in-a-row/)
- One market report says the simplified regime does not benefit third-country micro or small primary producers. — [APEDA market report](https://agriexchange.apeda.gov.in/MarketReport/EUR05082026129163155132.pdf)
- July 2025: Cameroon's Trade Minister said 99% of production areas have geolocation and traceability. Officials cite 24,800 farmers registered and more than 28,000 plots geolocated, mapped mainly by large exporters. — [Investir au Cameroun](https://m.investiraucameroun.com/news/i/14854/cameroon-says-cocoa-coffee-sector-99-traceable-ready-eu-deforestation-law); [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202507210357.html); [Food Business MEA](https://www.foodbusinessmea.com/cameroon-nears-full-traceability-of-cocoa-coffee-to-meet-eus-deforestation-regulation/)
- Exporters warn that a compliance cost of about 200 CFA/kg may overwhelm smaller cooperatives. CICC offers a free traceability platform and GEOSHARE parcel tool. — [AllAfrica, July 2025](https://allafrica.com/stories/202507210357.html)
- A national board MoU with a German firm on a traceability index left it unclear whether the data will flow into a national system or reach exporters. — search summary, [ModernGhana](https://www.modernghana.com/news/1418286/cameroon-nears-full-cocoa-traceability-as-eu-deadl.html)
- Readiness assessments exist from EFI and GIZ, and FAO has run farmer training. — [EFI EUDR preparedness check](https://efi.int/sites/default/files/files/flegtredd/Sustainable-cocoa-programme/Cocoa%20insights/EUDR%20Preparedness%20check%20CAM_EN.pdf); [GIZ forum](https://www.giz.de/en/regions/africa/cameroon/news/forum-cameroons-readiness-compliance-eudr); [FAO](https://www.fao.org/investment-centre/latest/news/detail/cocoa---coffee-festival--cameroonian-farmers-better-prepared-to-adapt-to-new-eu-regulation-on-deforestation/en)

### Inferences
- **EUDR scepticism.** The "99% traceable" claim deserves scrutiny. 24,800 registered farmers is small against Cameroon's cocoa smallholder base, which is commonly cited in the hundreds of thousands but was not verified here. Data sits with exporters and foreign vendors.
- **EUDR opportunity.** A farmer- or cooperative-owned, interoperable traceability layer is a well-defined, deadline-driven technical problem. It would combine polygon capture, satellite deforestation-cutoff checks against the 31 December 2020 baseline (Hansen/GFW, JRC TMF), mass-balance lot tracking and due-diligence statement export. It is globally relevant to Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Indonesia and Brazil.
- **Tontines and njangi as a data source.** Their repayment histories are an untapped reputation signal. Portable, consented "trust scores" built from rotating savings groups are a novel idea. Global analogues are ROSCAs worldwide, such as susu, chit funds and tandas.
- **Cross-border settlement.** Traders use hawala because PAPSS doesn't fit them. A trader-oriented XAF–NGN settlement and reputation network is a real gap.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 INS update of the informal share was found.
- No quantified tontine or njangi volumes were found. BEAC or COBAC data might hold them.
- No independent audit of Cameroon's 99% traceability claim was found.
- No verified smallholder count was found.
- The outcome of the April 2026 EUDR simplification review was not found.
- Cocoa export volumes and EU share were not retrieved.

## 5. Governance and public services (civil registration, administrative delays, disinformation, elections)

### Takeaway
About 1.67 million schoolchildren (29%) lack birth certificates and therefore cannot sit national exams. The civil registry is largely paper-based, and a 2025–2029 modernisation plan creates an institutional opening. Disinformation in the October 2025 presidential election was "hyper-connected" and AI-assisted, a growing and globally shared problem.

### Cited Findings
- About 1.67 million pupils (29.02% of schoolchildren) have no birth certificate, per the early-2024 statistical yearbook. Without one, a child can lose 5 to 6 years of schooling because they cannot take the CEP/FSLC. — [World Bank blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/nasikiliza/children-without-birth-certificate-in-cameroon)
- Al Jazeera (July 2026): more than 1.5 million children, about 30% of primary pupils, lack a birth certificate. — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/5/a-name-a-document-a-future-cameroons-fight-to-register-every-child)
- NRC: 30% of under-5s were without a certificate in 2021, with the Far North the most affected and about 190,000 unregistered primary pupils in the East (2023). The system is largely manual and paper-based, with records poorly stored and vulnerable to loss. Registration is legally due within 90 days and is free. — [NRC briefing 2026](https://www.nrc.no/globalassets/pdf/briefing-notes/securing-every-childs-identity/birth-registration-in-cameroon_2026.pdf)
- The Cameroon Strategic Plan for CRVS Modernization covers 2025–2029. UNICEF, MINDDEVEL and BUNEC ran a 2024 campaign targeting 76,000 pupils. — [UNICEF Cameroon](https://www.unicef.org/cameroon/universal-birth-registration-challenge-cameroon); [UNECA CRVS snapshot](https://apai-crvs.uneca.org/sites/default/files/resourcefiles/CRVS_CameroonSnapshot_e.pdf)
- 2025 election: the OECD AI incident monitor logged parties, including the SDF, using AI-generated images to inflate rally crowds. — [OECD.AI](https://oecd.ai/en/incidents/2025-10-11-8327)
- An ADISI-Cameroon, Data Cameroon and Data Check report called it the first "hyper-connected" presidential election, with unprecedented exposure to malicious content. — [Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/74553/en.html/details_2)
- A deepfake of Ibrahim Traoré circulated. Biya was declared re-elected with 53.66%. — [AllAfrica](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202510270672.html)

- In February 2025, MTN and Orange blocked Maurice Kamto's mobile money fundraising. This illustrates how payment rails intersect with politics. — [AllAfrica](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202502060303.html)

### Inferences
- **Civil registration** is a clear, quantified, child-welfare problem with a government strategy (2025–2029) and a donor ecosystem (UNICEF, NRC, World Bank) to plug into. The novel technology would be community-witnessed birth notification by health workers and the digitisation of damaged paper registers, using handwriting OCR on French and English records with deduplication. Records would be issued as verifiable credentials, which ties back to Section 1. Global analogues include the SDG 16.9 legal-identity goal, UNICEF's 150 million unregistered children worldwide (not verified here), MOSIP, and Nigeria's NIMC.
- **Disinformation.** The bigger opportunity is provenance and verification for local-language audio and video, plus WhatsApp-first fact-check triage, rather than generic fact-checking. Legal risk from false-news laws must be designed around.

### Gaps

- The full ADISI report numbers were not retrieved.

## 6. Energy (ENEO/SOCADEL outages) and climate/disaster

### Takeaway
Power outages ("délestages") are worsening in 2026 despite renationalisation. 65% of firms run generators, and the utility carries over 800 billion CFA in debt. The Far North faces recurrent mass flooding, with nearly 500,000 people affected in 2024. Outage-mapping and prediction tools for SMEs, and community flood early warning, are strong data-driven problems.

### Cited Findings
- ENEO was renationalised as SOCADEL in May 2026 after the state bought out Actis, which reports put at 78 billion CFA. It inherited more than 800 billion CFA in debt. In 2025 ENEO owed SONATREL about 273 billion CFA (about $450 million). — [AllAfrica, Aug 2026](https://allafrica.com/stories/202608030122.html); [AllAfrica, Nov 2025](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202511280552.html)
- The tariff has been frozen since 2012. The implicit subsidy is estimated at 70 to 100 billion CFA for 2025. — [AllAfrica, June 2026](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202606030730.html)
- GECAM, the employers' association, says 65% of firms run on generators. One hour of outage in Douala's industrial basin costs tens of millions of CFA. — [AllAfrica, June 2026](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202606030730.html); [Koaci, June 2026](https://www.koaci.com/article/2026/06/08/cameroun/economie/cameroun-energie-gaston-eloundou-essomba-impuissant-face-aux-delestages-qui-etranglent-le-pays_197348.html)
- In September 2026, GECAM publicly challenged SOCADEL over outages "paralysing" the economy. — [AllAfrica, Sept 2026](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202609130033.html)
- The Kribi gas and Dibamba plants stopped over unpaid bills in late 2024 and restarted in February 2025. — [AllAfrica, Feb 2025](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202502240254.html)
- Buea neighbourhoods went about a year without power until transformers were installed in December 2025. — [AllAfrica, Dec 2025](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202512260026.html)
- 2024 Far North floods affected about 459,000 to 500,000 people. Figures rose through the season, with 30 to 38 deaths, about 56,084 houses, 262 schools and 65 health centres affected. 98.7% of those affected were in Logone-et-Chari and Mayo-Danay. — [ReliefWeb/OCHA](https://reliefweb.int/report/cameroon/cameroon-floods-flash-update-01-6-september-2024); [UNICEF Flash Update No. 7](https://www.unicef.org/media/167366/file/Cameroon-Humanitarian-Flash-Update-No.7-(Floods-Far%20North)-31-January-2025.pdf.pdf); [CERF](https://cerf.un.org/sites/default/files/resources/CERF-CMR-24-RR-1427.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Outages.** Crowdsourced and IoT outage telemetry would serve both SMEs and the regulator (ARSEL). It would combine phone-charging events, cheap sensors and user reports to build a public "reliability ledger" by neighbourhood, plus generator fuel and solar sizing optimisation. Global analogues include Nigeria's and South Africa's load-shedding apps such as EskomSePush, which has millions of users.
- **Floods.** The Logone and Chari basin is a concentrated, predictable risk zone. It suits anticipatory-action triggers that combine satellite rainfall, river gauges and Chad and Nigeria upstream data with voice alerts in Fulfulde and Arabic Choa. Global analogues are Google Flood Hub and the WFP and OCHA anticipatory-action frameworks.

### Gaps
- No consolidated national figure for the economic cost of outages was found. ARSEL or Cour des comptes reports may have one.
- No SAIDI/SAIFI reliability indices were found for Cameroon.
- No climate-attribution study for the 2024 floods was found.
- No 2025 or 2026 flood figures were retrieved.

## 7. Global analogues (the bigger picture for judges)

### Takeaway
Almost every Cameroon problem above has a clear global twin. That lets a hackathon team pitch "built for Cameroon, generalisable to the world."

### Cited Findings
- Falsified medicines: WHO estimates 1 in 10 medical products in low- and middle-income countries is substandard or falsified. — [WHO](https://www.who.int/director-general/speeches/detail/who-director-general-s-opening-remarks-at-the-fighting-substandard-and-falsified-medicines-in-africa---23-august-2022)
- Mobile money is a $2 trillion-a-year global rail, with fraud a GSMA priority. — [GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/newsroom/press-release/mobile-money-accounted-for-2-trillion-in-transactions-in-2025-doubling-since-2021-as-active-accounts-continue-to-grow/)
- EUDR applies to all cocoa, coffee, palm, soy, rubber, cattle and wood exporters to the EU worldwide, with the December 2026 and June 2027 deadlines. — [EU Access2Markets](https://trade.ec.europa.eu/access-to-markets/en/news/delay-until-december-2026-and-other-developments-implementation-eudr-regulation)
- AI election disinformation was logged as an OECD AI incident. — [OECD.AI](https://oecd.ai/en/incidents/2025-10-11-8327)
- SIM-swap fraud is a regional problem, with examples in the DRC and Liberia. — [ENACT](https://enactafrica.org/enact-observer/weak-enforcement-measures-exacerbate-sim-swapping-in-the-drc); [Techpoint](https://techpoint.africa/news/surge-sim-swapping-attacks-liberia/)

### Inferences
Ranking by novelty, data-backing and global relevance. This is the researcher's judgement, not sourced.

1. **EUDR smallholder traceability and deforestation verification.** It has a hard deadline (December 2026 and June 2027), a quantified cost (200 CFA/kg), a global market and a clear technical core (geospatial plus supply-chain lot tracking).
2. **Cross-institution verifiable credentials** for diplomas, birth certificates and land documents. Fraud is documented (more than 1,000 MINDEF dismissals, 1,312 fake diplomas, 1.67 million children without certificates) and a CRVS modernisation plan exists for 2025–2029. Global analogues are EBSI, DigiLocker and MOSIP.
3. **Real-time mobile money scam and duress detection plus shared scam intelligence.** Fraud is 90% impersonation and 96% complaint-detected, with documented kidnapping-extortion cases.
4. **Point-of-sale medicine authentication** for informal outlets, where the substandard rate is 26.7% against 2.6% in formal ones.
5. **Voice AI in Pidgin and national languages** over official legal, administrative and health information. About half the population speaks Pidgin in some form, and there are 273 languages.
6. **Outage reliability ledger and anticipatory flood alerts.**

Existing attempts and why they fall short, from the evidence above:
- **CICC/GEOSHARE.** Mapping was done by exporters, and data ownership and integration are unclear.
- **Diploma-verification portal.** Limited to public-service recruitment and to single institutions.
- **MTN fraud.mtn.cm.** Reactive and complaint-based, run by a single operator.
- **PAPSS.** Low SME awareness, below 20%, and falling settlements.
- **DHIS2.** Aggregate only, not patient-level.
- **MINDCAF suspensions.** Have not stopped land fraud.

### Gaps
- No direct global-scale statistics were gathered for land-title insecurity (for example, World Bank or Prindex shares of people who feel tenure-insecure) or for unregistered births worldwide. These should be verified before being used in a pitch.
