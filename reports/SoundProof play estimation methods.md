# SoundProof wins by estimating shares, not plays

SoundProof should not try to count every play in Cameroon. It should estimate each song's **share** of bar plays from a stratified, rotating random panel of venues, the way GEMA does for German clubs, and then multiply that share by the pot. Shares are far more robust than totals. Any error that hits all songs equally, such as a quiet venue, a phone placed too far from the speaker, or a power cut, cancels out when one song's estimate is divided by the sum of all songs. That leaves three things to correct: errors that differ between songs or genres, false matches, and gaps in time coverage. The pipeline that does this has seven steps. It corrects each venue's detections for false positives, for detection probability measured with planted test plays, and for monitored hours. It weights venues by their sampling probability and computes ratio-type (Hájek) shares. It puts a venue-level bootstrap interval on every franc. It shrinks rarely heard songs toward their genre average. It holds unmatched and live music in escrow rather than handing that money to whoever happens to be in the catalogue. Finally it pays the point estimate, with a reconciliation hold-back. This is the standard collecting societies already accept: CISAC requires distribution on "actual usage or statistically valid sample" ([CISAC](https://www.cisac.org/compliance)), and PRS ranks census, then sampling, then proxy ([PRS](https://www.prsformusic.com/-/media/files/prs-for-music/membership/membership-policies/prs-distribution-policy/2prspolicydistributioncyclesandconceptsoct-22.pdf)). It is a clear improvement on SONACAM's October 2025 flat **27,000 FCFA** "advance" ([Mimi Mefo Info](https://mimimefoinfos.com/la-sonacam-indigne-les-artistes-camerounais-avec-27-000-fcfa-davance-de-droits-dauteur/)) and on the **100M FCFA beer-crate levy** of July 2026, which arrived with no usage data to split it ([Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/79271/en.html/copyrights-windfall-sonacam-secures-landmark-100-million-fcfa)). The two biggest unknowns are how well cheap phones hear makossa, bikutsi and coupé-décalé in loud bars, and how strongly playlists cluster by venue. No published study answers either, so SoundProof must measure both in a pilot. At the hackathon, the winning move is a seeded "digital twin" simulation that shows **misallocated FCFA** falling as each correction is switched on, next to a live phone detection and a Merkle-proof check.

## Collecting societies already pay on samples, and Cameroon pays on nothing

The global norm is a three-step hierarchy. PRS for Music names census (paying on every performance) as its preferred basis, sampling when a census is too expensive, and "analogy" (borrowing data from another medium, usually radio) when neither is feasible ([PRS policy](https://www.prsformusic.com/-/media/files/prs-for-music/membership/membership-policies/prs-distribution-policy/2prspolicydistributioncyclesandconceptsoct-22.pdf)). Pub background music is PRS's own example of a case where a statistically representative sample "would not be cost effective", so that money is paid on proxies. In an older policy edition, **53% of PRS public-performance revenue** was distributed by analogy, 29% by census and 18% by sample. Even for licensed venues, PRS collects data from "a statistically valid sample of UK venues" and matches it against broadcast data ([PRS](https://prsformusic.com/royalties/music-played-in-public-royalties)). SOCAN pays venue and background-music fees through radio pools, so airplay stands in for bar play ([SOCAN rules](https://www.socan.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Simplified-Distribution-Rules-2024.pdf)). ASCAP describes the same logic. It uses a census "whenever it is economically sensible" and otherwise "a sample survey designed to be a statistically accurate representation", and it follows the dollar: money from a medium is paid out on that medium's performances ([ASCAP via Harvard Berkman](https://cyber.harvard.edu/is02/readings/ascap.html)). SoundProof's argument is simple to state. Cheap phones lower the cost of each observation enough to move bar money from proxy or flat payment up to sampling, the middle rung of the hierarchy.

The closest working model is **GEMA's discotheque monitoring**. Boxes tap the DJ mixer output in a stratified random sample of dance floors split into **12 strata**, and the results are projected to the whole of Germany as each title's share of total play-time ([GEMA](https://gema.de/en/music-creators/monitoring/discotheque-monitoring); [GEMA info sheet](https://www.gema.de/fileadmin/user_upload/Musiknutzer/Informationen/information_diskothekenmonitoring.pdf)). GEMA calls the extrapolation "unbiased". Recent fact sheets describe about **1,600 dance floors a year, with 2 hours recorded per floor per operating week** ([GEMA FY2023](https://www.gema.de/documents/d/guest/infoblatt_dk_dk_vr_2023_de-pdf)). Critics still argue the sample leans toward major-label hits ([netzpolitik.org](https://netzpolitik.org/2015/black-box-discotheken-monitoring-gema-kooperiert-mit-yacast-gti-entwickelt-track-genaues-system/)). SOCAN supplies three further precedents. It weights each station "in direct proportion to the license fees received", it pays some services from a **21-day sample** per period, and it sets a credit value equal to the pool divided by total credits ([SOCAN](https://www.socan.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Simplified-Distribution-Rules-2024.pdf)). SOCAN also tells members honestly that a song can air on a sampled station "but was not captured for distribution". That is the long-tail cost of sampling, and SoundProof must budget for it. The frontier is moving toward direct data. PRS will start using play-by-play data from Soundtrack, covering about **7,000 UK premises**, in distributions from December 2026, partly because public-performance royalties in most countries are still paid on proxies ([Music Week](https://www.musicweek.com/publishing/read/new-prs-for-music-ceo-ben-mcowen-wilson-unveils-soundtrack-deal-to-improve-accuracy-of-royalties/095347)).

| Precedent | What it does | What SoundProof borrows |
|---|---|---|
| CISAC Professional Rules 18A–18C | Distribution on "actual usage or statistically valid sample"; procedures for unidentified uses ([CISAC](https://www.cisac.org/compliance)) | The legal standard to meet, plus an unmatched-music escrow |
| PRS hierarchy | Census > sample > analogy ([PRS](https://www.prsformusic.com/-/media/files/prs-for-music/membership/membership-policies/prs-distribution-policy/2prspolicydistributioncyclesandconceptsoct-22.pdf)) | Framing: moving the levy from flat/proxy payment to sampling |
| GEMA club monitoring | 12 strata, share of play-time, national projection ([GEMA](https://www.gema.de/fileadmin/user_upload/Musiknutzer/Informationen/information_diskothekenmonitoring.pdf)) | The core estimator design |
| SOCAN | Fee-weighted stations, 21-day samples, credit value = pool / credits ([SOCAN](https://www.socan.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Simplified-Distribution-Rules-2024.pdf)) | Weighting strata by levy money; the payout formula |
| ASCAP | "Follow the dollar" ([Berkman](https://cyber.harvard.edu/is02/readings/ascap.html)) | Keep the crate-levy pool on bar data, separate from radio |
| PRS + Soundtrack | Census data from 7,000 premises ([Music Week](https://www.musicweek.com/publishing/read/new-prs-for-music-ceo-ben-mcowen-wilson-unveils-soundtrack-deal-to-improve-accuracy-of-royalties/095347)) | Evidence that direct venue data is where the industry is heading |

Cameroon starts from a lower baseline than any of these. SONACAM's first distribution in 2018 split **154M FCFA among about 1,500 artists** using criteria based "notamment sur le degré de productivité des artistes" ([camer.be](https://camer.be/amp/culture/musique/cameroun-droits-dauteurs-154-millions-pour-les-artistes-cameroon.html)). A later round paid 25,000–300,000 FCFA to 2,040 artists ([Cameroon Tribune](https://cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/58312/fr.html/details_2)). The October 2025 round paid a flat 27,000 FCFA advance from a 40M FCFA envelope, through Express Union agencies ([Mimi Mefo Info](https://mimimefoinfos.com/la-sonacam-indigne-les-artistes-camerounais-avec-27-000-fcfa-davance-de-droits-dauteur/); [Cameroun24](https://cameroun24.net/article/70951-SONACAM___debut_des_paiements_de_la_repartition_du.html)). On 30 July 2026 SONACAM announced **100M FCFA collected from bars for the first time**, through a levy of **12 FCFA per crate** collected at source ([Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/79271/en.html/copyrights-windfall-sonacam-secures-landmark-100-million-fcfa)). Simple division puts that at roughly 8.3 million crates. None of the reporting mentions usage monitoring. Elsewhere in Africa, the documented practice is broadcaster logs (SAMRO), third-party airplay trackers (Radiomonitor, used by South Africa's MAUS tool), and repeated disputes over monitoring accuracy in Kenya. There, the band Elani gathered radio logs themselves after MCSK blamed "irregularities in monitoring airplay" ([Music In Africa](https://www.musicinafrica.net/magazine/maus-mark-stent-talks-about-new-sa-airplay-tracking-tool); [IP-Watch](https://www.ip-watch.org/2016/02/10/kenyan-musicians-escalate-fight-for-royalties/)). The research found **no working bar-monitoring network at any African collecting society**. The lesson from Kenya matters as much as the gap itself: monitoring data becomes political, so raw evidence must be open to every member who wants to check it.

## Seven corrections turn raw detections into a defensible pay key

### First decide what is being estimated

Before writing any formula, SONACAM's general assembly has to fix the **target quantity** (statisticians call it the *estimand*). The recommended target is: *each recording's share of music play-time in Cameroon's levy-paying bars during the period, with each region and venue type weighted by the levy money it generated.* That one sentence settles three choices. The first is **share, not count**: the pot is fixed, so only relative plays matter. The second is **play-time or duration-banded plays, not raw "hits"**. GEMA uses play duration, and SOCAN gives 1 credit for under a minute and 4 credits for 1:00–6:59 ([SOCAN](https://www.socan.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Simplified-Distribution-Rules-2024.pdf)), which stops a DJ's 20-second teaser counting the same as a full song. The third is **money-weighted strata**, the SOCAN station-weight idea applied to bars: a stratum that generated 30% of the crate money carries 30% of the weight. That is "follow the dollar" applied inside the levy. It needs crate sales by region and outlet type. Cameroon Tribune's French edition says the levy is collected through brewers' and distributors' logistics networks ([Cameroon Tribune FR](https://cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/79271/fr.html/details_2)), which suggests those figures exist, but this is an inference. If they do not, use venue counts instead and say so in the rules.

The notation is kept short. Venue *i* sits in stratum *h* (for example, city × venue type × size band). There are *N_h* venues in the stratum and *n_h* of them are sampled. *y_ij* is the number of detected plays of song *j* at venue *i*. The steps below each fix one specific way the raw count is wrong.

| Step | Fixes | Formula | In plain words |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. False-positive correction | Wrong matches | `y_ij × (1 − f̂_c)` | If audits show 3% of medium-confidence matches are wrong, keep 97% of them |
| 2. Detection correction | Missed plays | `÷ p̂_ij` | If the phone catches 60% of this genre's plays in this venue, divide by 0.6 |
| 3. Effort correction | Outages, partial nights | `× O_ib / e_ib` per hour-block *b* | If the phone was alive for 3 of 5 open hours, scale that block by 5/3 |
| 4. Design weights | Only some bars sampled | `w_i = N_h / n_h` (or 1/π_i) | Each monitored bar stands in for the unmonitored bars like it |
| 5. Hájek share | Fixed pot | `Ŝ_j = T̂_j / Σ_k T̂_k` | Song j's estimated plays divided by everyone's estimated plays |
| 6. Shrinkage + long-tail pool | Tiny, noisy counts | `(ỹ_j + α_g)/(E + β_g)`, Good–Turing `N1/N` | Do not trust one detection; lean on the genre average |
| 7. Payout | Uncertainty, disputes | `Pay_j = Pot_dist × Ŝ_j` + hold-back | Pay the best estimate now and reconcile at year end |

### Steps 1–3: correct each venue before adding anything up

The per-venue corrected count follows the "cue counting" template from passive acoustic monitoring of whales. In that field, the number of animals is estimated as detections × (1 − false-positive share) ÷ detection probability, scaled up for the share of the area covered, and biologists warn that "the most reliable estimates of f, p and r will come from data collected at the same time and region as n" ([Marques et al., Biological Reviews](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3743169/)). For SoundProof the "cue" is the play itself, so no conversion factor is needed:

```
ỹ_ij = Σ_b  [ y_ijb × (1 − f̂_c) / p̂_ijb ] × ( O_ib / e_ib )
```

Here *b* is an hour-block (for example six evening blocks × weekday/weekend), *O_ib* is the venue's opening minutes in that block, and *e_ib* is the minutes the phone was actually monitoring, taken from 60-second heartbeats. The effort expansion is done **within hour-blocks** because the song mix changes through the night. Scaling up a dead 1 a.m. block with 7 p.m. songs would bias the result. Three rules protect the estimate. A block with less than **25%** of its hours monitored is filled in from the same venue's same block on other nights rather than scaled up. A venue missing more than **50%** of the period is dropped, and its weight is shared among the other venues in its stratum (`w_i* = w_i × Σ_h w / Σ_h,responding w`). Any window where predicted detection *p̂* falls below **0.3** counts as "unmonitored", because dividing by a tiny *p̂* blows up the variance. By the delta method, `Var(N̂) ≈ y²·Var(p̂)/p⁴ + N(1−p)/p`. Occupancy models are known to behave well only above about *p* = 0.3 ([MacKenzie et al. 2002](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/5224176)), and N-mixture models "can produce unrealistically large estimates" when detection is low ([Dennis et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4406156/)).

Power cuts are not random. They cluster by city and time of night. If generator nights play different music, filling in from grid-powered nights is biased, so each phone should log its charging and power state and use it as a covariate. **Grid reliability should also be a stratification variable.** Within strata built that way, outages are closer to random.

### Steps 4–5: weight the venues, then take a ratio

Each sampled venue represents *N_h / n_h* venues like it. In general, the weight is the inverse of the probability that the venue was picked, *w_i = 1/π_i*. That is the **Horvitz–Thompson** idea, and it gives an unbiased national total ([Wikipedia: HT estimator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horvitz%E2%80%93Thompson_estimator)):

```
T̂_j = Σ_h (N_h / n_h) Σ_{i ∈ s_h} ỹ_ij          (national plays of song j)
Ŝ_j = T̂_j / Σ_k T̂_k                              (Hájek / ratio share)
Ŝ_j(money) = Σ_h (M_h / M) × Ŝ_j,h                 (levy-weighted version)
```

The share divides one weighted total by another that uses the same weights. That is the Hájek or ratio form ([Wikipedia: Design effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_effect)), and it brings the most important property in this report: **anything that multiplies every song's count in a venue by the same factor cancels out.** Suppose a phone in a noisy club catches half of all plays, whatever the song. The club's counts are halved, but so is everyone else's in that club, and the club's song mix is unchanged. Likewise, if the national venue count is wrong by 20% in every stratum, the shares do not move. Two cautions apply. Getting the *relative* sizes of strata wrong does still shift shares. And if loud clubs (with low detection) systematically play a different genre, the venue factor no longer cancels. For that reason Step 2 divides by each venue's own *p̂* before anything is added up. The last line above is the money-weighted version: compute each song's share within stratum *h*, then average across strata using the stratum's share of levy money *M_h/M*. With PPS sampling (picking venues with probability proportional to a size measure such as crate volume or opening hours), set `π_i = n_h·x_i / X_h`.

### Why song-specific detection is the error that matters

Common detection errors cancel. **Song-specific** ones do not. If makossa is detected at 80% of the rate of everything else, its raw share is pushed down in proportion. The notes give a worked example. A genre with a true 30% share and relative detection 0.8 shows up at `0.3×0.8 / (0.3×0.8 + 0.7) = 25.5%`. That is a **4.5-point error, worth 4.5M FCFA** of a 100M pot, more than 160 flat 27,000 FCFA payouts. The fix is to divide by *genre-level* (or song-level, shrunk toward the genre) detection probability from calibration: `Ŝ_j = (y_j / p̂_j) / Σ_k (y_k / p̂_k)`. Only *relative* detection matters. Even if calibration gets the absolute level wrong by a constant, the shares still come out right. That is why a modest calibration budget pays for itself: **genre-level correction matters more than total-level correction.**

### Step 6: stop rare songs from winning or losing a lottery

Most catalogue songs will have 0–2 detections in a period. One lucky detection in a monitored bar, multiplied by a weight of 50, makes a song look like a mid-tier hit. One unlucky miss makes a song that really was played look like zero. **Empirical-Bayes shrinkage** pulls each small count toward the average for its genre (or artist), and it pulls harder the less evidence there is:

```
λ̂_j = (ỹ_j + α_g) / (E + β_g)          shrinkage weight toward own data = E/(E + β_g)
Ŝ_j = (ỹ_j + a·m_j) / (Ñ + a)          Dirichlet–multinomial share version
```

*α_g* and *β_g* are fitted across all the songs in genre *g*. In plain words, a song with thousands of detections is paid almost exactly on its own data, while a song with one detection is paid mostly on "what a typical song in this genre earns given that it was heard at all". After shrinking, **renormalise so the shares still sum to the pot.** Shrinkage moves money from rare songs that look high to rare songs that look like zero within the same genre. That is defensible as the best estimate given the evidence, but it must be written into the rules and explained.

Next, estimate how much play belongs to catalogue songs that were **never detected**. Good–Turing estimates that missing mass as `p0 = N1/N`, the share of observations that are songs seen exactly once ([Wikipedia: Good–Turing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good%E2%80%93Turing_frequency_estimation)). Pay that mass into a **long-tail pool** and distribute it by a prior: registration, radio plays, or an equal amount per verified-active registered song. Chao1 (`S_obs + f1(f1−1)/(2(f2+1))`) estimates how many distinct catalogue songs are really being played, and therefore how many claimants to expect in that pool ([scikit-bio](https://scikit.bio/docs/dev/generated/skbio.diversity.alpha.chao1.html)). If N1/N exceeds about 10%, the panel is too small to resolve the tail, which is itself a signal to enlarge it.

### Uncatalogued, live and DJ music go to escrow, not to the catalogue

The fingerprinter can flag "music present, no match". The **unmatched share of music-time is measured directly**, which is better than any statistical guess. Splitting that money pro rata among catalogued songs would quietly pay registered artists for unregistered artists' plays. The fairer rule is to put the measured unmatched share in **escrow**. Keep the unmatched fingerprint hashes (never audio). When a work is registered within a claims window, re-match it against the stored hashes and pay it retroactively. Release anything unclaimed under a published rule. This mirrors CISAC's expectation of procedures for unidentified uses ([CISAC](https://www.cisac.org/compliance)) and SOCAN's experience that unregistered works are simply not captured ([SOCAN](https://www.socan.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Simplified-Distribution-Rules-2024.pdf)). Live renditions and heavily edited DJ versions are a separate "cue class", because fingerprints are version-specific and "not expected to generalize to live recordings" ([Wang 2003](https://www.princeton.edu/~cuff/ele201/files/Wang03-shazam.pdf)). Measure their share from human audits of a random sample of unmatched windows and pay them from a live/DJ pool. Divide that pool by the audit's song identifications, or, later, by a second cover-song detector with its own calibrated *p*.

### Step 7: pay the best estimate, keep a reserve, never pay only "significant" songs

Pay each rights holder the pot multiplied by the point estimate. For a fixed pot, this minimises the expected error, sums exactly to the pot, and is unbiased over many periods, so every rights holder gets their true share on average. The tempting alternative, "pay only songs whose interval excludes zero", is **biased against the tail** and should be rejected. The layout below is a recommendation for a 100M FCFA pot. Every percentage is a design choice for the general assembly to adopt in advance, not a sourced standard, and the administration fee is left blank because the research found no source for it.

| Line | Rule (recommendation) | Illustrative share of 100M |
|---|---|---|
| Administration | Per SONACAM statutes (Law 2000/011 art. 78) | not specified |
| Unmatched escrow | Measured unmatched share of music-time; retro-matched on registration | e.g. 10–25M, measured |
| Live/DJ pool | Measured from audits | measured |
| Solidarity floor | 2–5% top-slice, equal per verified-active registered song or artist | 2–5M |
| Long-tail pool | Good–Turing missing mass N1/N | measured |
| Hold-back | Pay 85–90% now; release 10–15% at annual reconciliation on pooled 12-month data | 10–15% of remainder |
| De-minimis | Amounts under ~5,000 FCFA carry forward rather than being dropped | — |
| Main distribution | Pot × Ŝ_j, shrunk and renormalised | the rest |

Rolling a three-period average of Ŝ cuts variance roughly threefold for stable songs but lags new hits, so pair it with the hold-back rather than using it alone. When a rights holder disputes a result, that should trigger extra targeted monitoring whose results feed *future* periods. Never re-weight a single venue retroactively, because that invites gaming. A 1% share of the main pool is about 1M FCFA, or 37 times the October 2025 flat advance. That one sentence explains to an artist why measurement matters.

### Every franc gets an interval from a venue-level bootstrap

Treating every play as independent (a multinomial interval) badly understates uncertainty. Plays cluster: the same DJ and the same USB playlist produce hundreds of correlated plays. The interval has to come from resampling **venues**, not plays. The recommended method is the Rao–Wu rescaled bootstrap, which is consistent for HT-type estimators under complex designs ([Wikipedia: HT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horvitz%E2%80%93Thompson_estimator)). Repeat B ≥ 1,000 times. In each repetition, resample *n_h − 1* venues with replacement within each stratum, rescale the weights by `n_h/(n_h − 1)` × the number of times each venue was drawn, **also redraw the planted-play calibration trials and the false-positive audit sample** so that detection uncertainty flows through, and recompute every Ŝ_j. The 5th–95th percentiles give a 90% range. The quick analytic version is the linearised variance `v(Ŝ_j) ≈ Σ_h (1−f_h)·n_h/(n_h−1)·Σ_i (u_ij − ū_hj)²`, with `u_ij = w_i(ỹ_ij − Ŝ_j·ỹ_i·)/T̂_·`. R's `survey::svyratio` can serve as a cross-check. To a non-statistician, the output reads like this: "Song X: estimated 1.8M FCFA (90% range 1.4M–2.3M)", or "very likely top 10" when more than 90% of bootstrap draws rank it in the top ten.

### Radio is a separate census, not a top-up

Monitoring clean digital radio streams is close to a census of the stations covered, with detection near 1. It belongs in **its own pot**, following PRS's separate sections and ASCAP's follow-the-dollar principle. It should not be mixed into bar counts, because a radio play and a bar play are different uses paid by different payers. Radio is still useful inside the bar estimator, as the prior *m_j* in the shrinkage step and as a covariate for regional estimates (Fay–Herriot small-area models, `γ_d·θ̂_d + (1 − γ_d)·x_d'β̂`). Whether bar playlists actually look like radio playlists in Cameroon is an open hypothesis. A pilot should test it directly, and if they differ, that difference is SoundProof's whole reason to exist. Radio can be gamed too: a monitored stream URL may not match what goes out over the air. Spot-checks against off-air recordings close that gap. Note also that Cameroon Tribune reports only CRTV "really" pays copyright among audiovisual media ([Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/76554/en.html/collecte-du-droit-dauteur-une-assiette-dusagers-elargir)).

## Phones hear between 5% and 97% of songs, so SoundProof must measure its own ears

Published accuracy depends heavily on conditions, and lab numbers flatter. Wang's original Shazam paper, tested against pub noise, reached 50% recognition at about **−9 dB SNR for 15-second clips**. Adding GSM-style compression moved that point to about **−3 dB**, a loss of 6–7 dB of noise margin. A significant match needed only 1–2% of hashes to survive ([Wang 2003](https://www.princeton.edu/~cuff/ele201/files/Wang03-shazam.pdf)). Neural fingerprints reach 92–96% top-1 at 5–10 s against 100,000 songs under synthetic noise ([Chang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11910)). The one study that actually played music through a speaker into a device microphone with competing noise found something much harsher ([Nikou & Giannakopoulos 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.06070)):

| System (real speaker-to-mic test) | 5 s query, low / mid / high distortion | 15 s query, low / mid / high |
|---|---|---|
| NAFP, original training | 48.8 / 32.3 / **9.0%** | 70.7 / 56.0 / 17.5% |
| NAFP, band-limited augmentation | 62.3 / 44.6 / 24.8% | 78.5 / 72.1 / 45.3% |
| BEATs + transfer learning | 86.1 / 68.5 / 34.1% | **97.0 / 90.2 / 56.5%** |

On realistic background-music broadcast audio, open tools such as audfprint and Panako recall only **2–15% of music seconds** at high precision, and "none of the algorithms obtain a F1-score above 47%" ([BAF, ISMIR 2022](https://zenodo.org/record/7372162/files/000109.pdf)). Time-stretching is the classic weakness. Dejavu identifies only **25%** of time-stretched queries, against 96% for a fine-tuned music foundation model ([arXiv 2511.05399](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.05399v1)). Panako 2.0 raised 10%-speed-up recognition from 18% to 83% ([Panako 2.0](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2021/latebreaking/000039.pdf)). That matters wherever DJs pitch or tempo-shift tracks. Even the music/no-music gate leaks. Google's purpose-built on-device detector reached only **75.5% recall** at about one false trigger per 20 minutes ([Now Playing 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10958)). Since music is almost always present in a bar, SoundProof should set the gate permissively or run fingerprinting on a fixed duty cycle without a gate. Vendors publish coverage (ACRCloud claims 50,000+ stations and 150M+ tracks), not independently verified venue accuracy ([ACRCloud](https://acrcloud.com/broadcast-monitoring)). So any "99%" claim should be read as referring to clean feeds.

Two facts help a great deal. First, a song lasts 3–4 minutes, and a phone gets many query windows per song. If each 10-second window had only a 30% chance of matching, and windows were independent, the chance of at least two consistent matches across 18 windows would be about 99% (our arithmetic). Windows are not independent in practice: a loud crowd or a distant phone degrades the whole song. That is exactly why per-song detection has to be **measured**, not derived. Second, the Android audio source matters. The `VOICE_RECOGNITION` source must, by default, disable noise reduction and automatic gain control and be roughly flat from 100 Hz to 4 kHz. `UNPROCESSED` is better but optional, and `VOICE_COMMUNICATION` applies echo cancellation and gain control, so it should be avoided ([Android 16 CDD](https://source.android.com/docs/compatibility/16/android-16-cdd); [Android AudioSource](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/media/MediaRecorder.AudioSource)). Below 100 Hz the microphone is only "strongly recommended" to stay within ±20 dB, so bass-heavy mixes will be matched mostly on their mid and high bands. Fully on-device matching is feasible: Google's Now Playing needs under 3 KB per song for tens of thousands of songs and uses under 1% of battery a day ([Now Playing](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10958)).

False positives scale with catalogue size and with the number of queries. Google notes that growing from tens of thousands to tens of millions of songs means "a thousand times as many songs which could give a false positive", and it removes them by requiring sequence agreement ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/googles-next-generation-music-recognition/)). Wang sets the threshold from the score distribution of the best-scoring *wrong* match, aiming for a 0.1% or 0.01% false-positive rate ([Wang 2003](https://www.princeton.edu/~cuff/ele201/files/Wang03-shazam.pdf)). A phone that queries all night runs thousands of queries a day, so SoundProof should report **false detections per device-hour**, not per query. It should also require k-of-n agreement: the same track ID with time offsets advancing at about real time across at least 2–3 consecutive windows. **Genre bias is the biggest unknown.** No study measures fingerprint accuracy on makossa, bikutsi, afrobeats or coupé-décalé. Benchmarks use mostly Western or instrumental music, and BAF's references are 93% production music ([BAF](https://zenodo.org/record/7372162/files/000109.pdf)). Shared beats and riddims, crowd sing-alongs, and multiple versions (radio edit, extended, regional re-upload) all plausibly lower accuracy or cause confusion between tracks.

### A seven-part calibration protocol

The protocol below adapts published methods into a routine for SoundProof. It is our synthesis, not a collecting-society standard, because none was found.

| Component | How | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Lab baseline | Re-record a genre-stratified track set through a bar PA onto the deployment phone at 1/3/5/10 m, with bar noise at +10/+5/0/−5 dB; use the public benchmark code ([magcil repo](https://github.com/magcil/deep-audio-fingerprinting-benchmark)) | Recall by genre, distance, SNR and query length |
| Planted plays in situ | At installation and then monthly, staff play a randomised list of known tracks at normal volume, across busy and quiet hours and in full and snippet lengths | `p̂ = detected / planted` with Wilson/Beta interval |
| Detection model | `logit p = β0 + noise floor + placement + genre + loudness + duration + device + time of night + venue random effect` | *p̂* for every real play window, from covariates the phone logs |
| Gold-standard venues | 5–10 venues with DJ software exports or a mixer line-in recorder | True recall, precision and timing error |
| Two-phone recapture | Two phones in different positions; Chapman `N̂ = (n_A+1)(n_B+1)/(m+1) − 1`, `p̂_A = m/n_B` ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_and_recapture)) | Drift monitor without ground truth (biased low when both phones miss the same plays) |
| False-positive audit | Run the matcher on hours of out-of-catalogue, talk and TV audio; hand-check a random 1–2% of matches (about 200 per stratum) by confidence band | `f̂` per band; false matches per device-hour |
| Health checks | Daily detection rate per device, unmatched-music fraction, mic level; recalibrate on any change of phone model, firmware, placement, catalogue size or model version | Drift alarms |

Planted plays set the **absolute level**. The two-phone check catches **drift**. It cannot be the only correction, because both phones tend to fail on the same quiet tracks and loud moments, which biases the estimated total downward.

## About 100 venue-months buys ±20% on a song with a 1% share

Precision comes from the **number of venues**, not the hours per venue. Plays in one bar look alike: the same DJ, the same playlist. Statisticians measure this with the *intraclass correlation* ρ. The design effect `Deff = 1 + (m̄ − 1)ρ` says how much less information clustered plays carry than independent ones ([Wikipedia: Design effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_effect)). For a relative margin *r* on a share *S*, the effective number of plays needed is `n_eff ≈ z²(1 − S)/(S·r²)`. The worked figures below use the notes' illustrative values, about 2,000 plays per venue per month and ρ = 0.01, which give Deff ≈ 21. Both numbers must be replaced with pilot measurements.

| Song share | Plays needed at ±20% (if independent) | × Deff ≈ 21 | Venue-months at 2,000 plays each |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5% (a top hit) | ~1,800 | ~38,000 | **~19** |
| 1% (a strong song) | ~9,500 | ~200,000 | **~100** |
| 0.1% (a typical catalogue song) | ~96,000 | ~2,000,000 | **~1,000** |

The table carries the key strategic message. **No affordable panel measures the long tail precisely in a single quarter.** About 35 phones running continuously for a quarter gives roughly 100 venue-months, which is enough for the top few dozen songs. Everything below that depends on pooling across periods, shrinkage, the long-tail pool and the floor. That design is the honest answer to the question "will small artists be treated fairly?". The panel should still be tuned. **Neyman allocation** puts more venues in strata whose play mix varies more, `n_h = n·N_h·S_h / Σ N_h·S_h` ([Wikipedia: Neyman allocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neyman_allocation)). Kish's formula `1 + CV²(w)` gives the precision cost of very unequal weights. Phones record continuously within opening hours, so time inside a monitored venue is a census, not a sample. Human audits are the exception and should be time-sampled with a systematic random start across hour-blocks.

The recommended panel follows a Nielsen-style rotation. Strata are built from city (Douala, Yaoundé, regional towns, rural), venue type (bar/snack-bar, nightclub, maquis/restaurant, hotel), size band and grid reliability. About **a quarter of the venues rotate out each quarter**. That keeps enough overlap between periods to estimate change precisely, covers more distinct bars over a year, limits the time a venue owner has to learn the system, and keeps venue identities confidential. If a licensing registry exists, weight the panel to its known city × type totals (raking). Nielsen's experience is a warning: thousands of programmes with 100,000+ viewers are "too small to be detected by panel-based measurement", which is why it now blends panels with other data ([Nielsen](https://www.nielsen.com/insights/2025/improve-data-quality-big-data-people-panels)). SoundProof's equivalent blend is radio priors plus artist-submitted evidence. GEMA monitors about 1,600 dance floors ([GEMA](https://www.gema.de/documents/d/guest/infoblatt_dk_dk_vr_2023_de-pdf)). A 100M FCFA pot justifies far fewer, and the table shows exactly what that trade-off costs.

## Every monitored bar is worth 100 bars to a fraudster

A sampled panel concentrates value. If 1% of bars are monitored, each bought play inside the panel is multiplied by a weight of about 100. Fraud follows royalties wherever plays become money. Michael Smith pleaded guilty in March 2026 to wire-fraud conspiracy for streaming hundreds of thousands of AI tracks with bots, and agreed to forfeit **$8,091,843.64** ([US Attorney SDNY](https://justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/north-carolina-man-pleads-guilty-music-streaming-fraud-aided-artificial-intelligence-0)). He reportedly spread streams thinly so that no single track looked anomalous ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/111787-man-who-used-1000-bots-stream-ai-songs.html)). Deezer reports that up to **85%** of streams on fully AI-generated tracks were fraudulent in 2025, against about 8% across its whole catalogue ([TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2026/04/20/deezer-says-44-of-songs-uploaded-to-its-platform-daily-are-ai-generated/)). On the broadcast side, the FCC opened a 2025 inquiry into "covert manipulation of airplay" at iHeartMedia, closed by consent decree ([FCC](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-683A1.pdf)). The research found no published case of fraud against a sampled venue panel. That is a known unknown, not evidence that it does not happen.

| Threat | Example | Defence (recommendation built on cited practice) |
|---|---|---|
| Panel-targeted looping | Promoter pays a monitored bar's DJ to loop a track | Secret, rotating panel; commit-reveal of the selection seed; repeat-interval and single-artist-share anomaly checks; cap any single venue's contribution to an artist's estimate |
| Bar-owner capture | Phone moved or unplugged for rivals' sets | Placement checks via mic level and two-phone agreement; unannounced shadow devices and mystery-shopper visits as an audit stratum |
| Audio injection | Phone in a quiet room fed a playlist at 4 a.m. | Liveness features (crowd noise floor, spectral room signature); plays outside opening hours flagged |
| Device emulation / forgery | Rooted phone or emulator forging signed records | Hardware-backed key attestation at enrolment; Play Integrity `MEETS_DEVICE_INTEGRITY` or better ([Android](https://developer.android.com/google/play/integrity/improvements)); per-observation signatures from StrongBox/TEE keys |
| Sybil venues | Fake or duplicate "venues" | SoundProof-issued managed phones in kiosk mode; venues drawn from a frame, never self-enrolled |
| Catalogue attacks | Soundalikes, or claiming folklore recordings | Registration checks; disputed-ownership escrow; folklore is national heritage under Law 2000/011 art. 5 ([OAPI](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)) |
| Radio gaming | Monitored stream differs from over-the-air broadcast | Off-air spot recordings; radio kept in its own pot |
| Insider tampering | Staff edit rows or weights | Certificate Transparency-style Merkle log with signed tree heads and consistency proofs ([RFC 9162](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9162)); versioned, published rules; independent annual statistical and security audits |

Two subtleties belong in the design documents. First, the Merkle log proves that nothing was changed **after** capture. It does not prove the capture itself was honest. Attestation, liveness checks, corroboration and audits cover that gap. Second, strict device-integrity gating has a cost. Google tightened its strong verdicts in 2025, reducing them by about 14.5% ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/google/play/integrity/improvements)), and many cheap phones may fail. That is another reason for a managed fleet of identical phones. Caps and winsorising (clipping extreme values) limit what looping can earn, at the price of a small, disclosed bias. Spotify shows the deterrent half of the design: since April 2024 it charges labels and distributors a fee for flagrant artificial streaming and demonetises flagged streams ([Spotify](https://artists.spotify.com/en/artificial-streaming)). SONACAM's equivalent is to withhold a flagged rights holder's share pending review, under a sanction rule written into the rules in advance, so that it holds up in court.

There is a real tension between the public log and panel secrecy. The fix is **commit-reveal**. Before each period, publish a hash of the venue list and the random seed. During the period, put only salted venue codes in the public log. After the period closes, reveal the seed and the venue list. Anyone can then check that the sample was drawn randomly and not cherry-picked, but nobody knew which bars were monitored while it mattered.

## Fairness means protecting the tail, the regions and the unregistered

Changing the formula moves less money than people expect. France's CNM found that switching streaming to user-centric payment would lift the bottom tier's share only from **7.7% to 8.1%**, with artists ranked below 10,000 gaining under €10 on average ([Music in Africa](https://www.musicinafrica.net/node/150521)). For SoundProof the larger fairness risks are **coverage** and **small-count noise**, not the choice of formula. Coverage bias has concrete forms here. Douala and Yaoundé could be over-represented. The Anglophone North-West and South-West could be under-sampled for security reasons. Music played mostly at funerals, in churches or at home would be missed. Local-language and traditional recordings may be absent from the catalogue, and fingerprinting only matches what is registered. The mitigations are stratification with **published weights**, a catalogue-registration drive before launch, the unmatched escrow, and reporting **signed bias by genre, region and language** from the simulation. A regulator will ask whether one genre is systematically underpaid, and SoundProof should be ready with the number.

The transition also needs managing. SONACAM's history of flat payouts means a pure switch to pro-rata creates visible losers, mostly less-played artists, who will push back. The recommendation is to phase it in. For example, distribute a growing fraction of the levy by estimated plays each year, keep a declining flat or solidarity component, and publish both. The governance climate makes procedure decisive. In 2021 the artists' association Adang won a court decision against MINAC, the oversight commission and SONACAM's board over a contested distribution ([Cameroon Tribune](https://cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/49423/fr.html/details_2)). SONACAM's leadership was re-elected in 2026 against 45 opposition votes ([AllAfrica](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202605010511.html)). On 30 October 2026 Cameroon Tribune argued that collective management would improve if royalties were distributed "selon des critères clairs, définis à l'avance" ([Cameroon Tribune](https://cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/82152/fr.html/details_2)). That phrase is SoundProof's fairness brief. The method, code specification and intervals should be published. Each artist should be able to see their own detections through Merkle inclusion proofs. There should be a 3–6 month claims window with an evidence standard (artist logs, radio playlists), followed by a human appeals board, then escalation to the oversight commission, the arbitration commission and the courts.

## Law 2000/011 asks for "exact programmes", and a valid sample is the closest workable substitute

Cameroon's copyright law gives the strongest local argument. **Article 63(1) of Law No. 2000/011** requires users to supply the collecting society with "les programmes exacts des utilisations auxquelles elles procèdent et tous les éléments documentaires indispensables à la répartition des droits" ([OAPI-hosted text](https://oapi.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Loi_19decembre_2000Cameroun.pdf)). Bars supply nothing of the kind, which is why flat keys exist. A documented, statistically valid sensor sample comes far closer to the statute's aim of distribution by actual use. Other provisions frame the process. Art. 61 makes commercial users pay remuneration for public use of phonograms and splits it "par moitié" between performers and producers. Art. 62 has the tariff set with users or by an arbitration commission. Art. 75(2) provides one collecting society per category. Art. 78(2) requires revenue to be used per statutes approved by the Minister. Art. 79 requires changes to "règles de perception et de répartition des droits" to be communicated to the Minister **at least one month before** the general assembly examines them, and lets the Minister inspect "sur pièces et sur place". Decree 2001/956/PM adds that MINAC approval is required, lasts five years and can be suspended, and that the arbitration commission is chaired by a judge. It contains no distribution rules, so the method is SONACAM's to define ([WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/491116)). Defensibility therefore depends on four things. The rules must be adopted in advance by the general assembly and filed under Art. 79. The method must be published with error bounds. It must be independently audited. And it needs a working complaints path. The EU's Directive 2014/26/EU is a useful benchmark for accurate and timely distribution, transparency reports and complaints, though it is not binding in Cameroon and its wording was not re-verified this session ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32014L0026)). The WIPO Good Practice Toolkit (updated January 2025) is explicitly non-normative ([WIPO](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/copyright/w/news/2025/update-of-wipo-good-practice-toolkit-for-collective-management-organizations)).

One legal question changes **what** SoundProof measures. Fingerprinting identifies *recordings*. Authors' rights are paid on *works*, and Art. 61 neighbouring rights go to performers and producers. The 2009-era bar mechanism had the music collecting society collect as agent for the others under a sharing key ([Cameroon Tribune](https://cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/35250/fr.html/details_2)). The rules must therefore state whose levy this is, and the catalogue must map each recording to its works and performers. **Metadata quality becomes a fairness and fraud issue** in its own right. The research found no court ruling anywhere that directly tests sample-based distribution. That is an open gap, not a green light.

Privacy is governed by **Law No. 2024/017 of 23 December 2024**, which became enforceable on **23 June 2026** after an 18-month transition and created an independent data protection authority ([Droit Médias & Finance](https://droitmediasfinance.com/index.php/actualites/droit-tech-fintech/1297-cameroun-la-loi-sur-la-protection-des-donnees-a-caractere-personnel-entre-en-vigueur-ce-23-juin-2026); [Digital Business Africa](https://www.digitalbusiness.africa/cameroun-lautorite-de-protection-des-donnees-a-caractere-personnel-creee/)). Practitioner commentary says it covers people residing in or merely *in transit* through Cameroon, requires prior authorisation for processing (Art. 19) and for transfers abroad (Art. 32, numbering uncertain), and generally requires consent ([CIO Mag](https://cio-mag.com/?p=59861)). Reported fines reach **1 billion FCFA** ([Digital Business Africa](https://www.digitalbusiness.africa/cameroun-bientot-des-peines-damende-d-un-milliard-de-francs-cfa-pour-violation-des-donnees-personnelles/)). The official gazette text was not read, so article numbers and the available legal bases need a lawyer's check. A fingerprint of a commercial recording, plus a venue code and a timestamp, is arguably not personal data. Raw audio of patrons is, and so are device and operator identifiers. The design has to keep the first and never create the second. Compute lossy landmark hashes on the device. Discard the audio buffer at once. Never upload or store audio. Never run speech recognition. Keep unmatched hashes only from windows the music gate classified as music. Put a visible notice in every bar, following the EDPB's 2026 call for clear recording signals, on-device processing and limited retention for devices with ambient microphones ([EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-11/20261013_edpb_012026_statement_smart-glasses_en.pdf)). Sign a consent contract with each venue owner. Limit how long logs are kept. Host data in Cameroon, or obtain transfer authorisation before using foreign cloud. Register with the new authority before deployment, run an impact assessment as good practice, and appoint a data protection officer.

## Prove it on a digital twin before asking for a single franc

The validation standard comes from ecology, where estimators are trusted only after simulations show their bias and interval coverage against a known truth ([Royle 2004](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/5224798); [MacKenzie 2002](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/5224176)). SoundProof should build a seeded **digital twin** of Cameroon's bar universe. It needs a venue frame by stratum with lognormal activity. Song popularity should follow a Zipf (power-law) curve, with genre × city × hour interactions and venue-specific playlist affinity to produce realistic clustering. Plays are Poisson per venue-hour. Detection is logistic in noise, genre, duration and device. Outages follow a Markov on/off process correlated by city and evening. The twin also needs a catalogue gap, a live/DJ fraction by venue type, false positives by confidence band, and injected frauds. Run the full pipeline across about 500 Monte Carlo repetitions and score it against the true logs.

| Metric | Definition | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Misallocated FCFA** | `½ × Pot × Σ_j |Ŝ_j − S_j|` (total-variation distance × pot) | The headline number: francs that went to the wrong people |
| Weighted relative error | `Σ|err| / ΣS` by popularity decile (avoid plain MAPE for tiny shares) | Shows how error grows down the tail |
| Rank accuracy | Spearman/Kendall; top-20 precision and recall | "Did we get the hits right?" |
| Interval coverage | Share of songs whose 90% range contains the truth (target ≈ 0.90) | Whether the stated uncertainty is honest |
| Fairness | Signed bias by genre, region and language; Gini of absolute payment errors across artists | The regulator's question |
| Fraud recall | Share of injected loops, injections and cloned keys caught; FCFA leaked | The security claim |

The most persuasive output is an **ablation table**: naive raw counts, then design weights added, then the effort correction, then genre-level detection correction, then shrinkage. Each step's drop in misallocated FCFA makes its value concrete. After the simulation, run a field check in 5–10 venues with DJ exports or line-in recordings, to measure real *p* and *f* and test the simulator's assumptions against reality. Publish both. The Gini-of-errors metric is a proposal, not an established standard.

## Conclusion

The research changes what "accurate" means for SoundProof. The goal is not to catch more plays. It is to correct only the errors that differ between songs, and to be open about the rest. Because of the Hájek ratio, most of what looks frightening in a Cameroonian bar (noise, distance, power cuts, an unknown number of bars) largely cancels. Calibration effort should go almost entirely to genre- and version-specific detection, the one error that moves millions of francs. The sample-size arithmetic leads to an equally important conclusion: no affordable panel can measure a typical catalogue song precisely in one quarter. Fairness to the long tail is therefore a **rules** problem, solved by pooling, shrinkage, escrow and a floor adopted in advance, not a sensor problem.

The design also turns SONACAM's weakest point into evidence. Flat payouts have a large, hidden misallocation. A sampled estimate has a smaller misallocation that is published, can be audited and is bounded by intervals. Combined with Article 63's demand for exact programmes and CISAC's acceptance of valid samples, that gives SONACAM a lawful and checkable path from 27,000 FCFA for everyone to payment by what bars actually played. The pilot has to answer two questions no source could: how cheap phones hear makossa, bikutsi and coupé-décalé in real bars, and how strongly playlists cluster by venue.
