# Build Cameroon's missing royalty data layer

The strongest first-place bet for a Cameroonian team at the CIMFEST26 hackathon is a **verifiable music-usage network**. Phones in bars, at events and beside radios fingerprint the music that is actually playing. Each detection goes into a tamper-evident public log, and a deterministic engine splits real royalty pots, starting with SONACAM's new **12 FCFA-per-beer-crate levy**, by measured plays instead of flat handouts. We call it **TamTam Ledger** (our recommendation, not a sourced name). The problem it fixes is concrete and current. In October 2025 Cameroon's music collecting society paid every artist the same **27,000 FCFA "advance" out of a 40M FCFA envelope** ([Mimi Mefo Info](https://mimimefoinfos.com/la-sonacam-indigne-les-artistes-camerounais-avec-27-000-fcfa-davance-de-droits-dauteur/)). In July 2026 it collected **100M FCFA from bars for the first time** through the crate levy, which comes with no usage data to divide it by ([Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/79271/en.html/copyrights-windfall-sonacam-secures-landmark-100-million-fcfa)). That makes this a data and trust problem, not a "the internet is slow" problem. It scores on all three criteria CIMFEST used in 2025: innovation, feasibility and long-term industry impact ([Aftown](https://news.aftown.com/cimfest-2025-bat-des-records/)). It can be built in 48 hours with Claude Code on mature open-source fingerprinting, and it carries a real systems story: edge capture, noise-robust matching, statistical sampling, anti-fraud and an auditable ledger. It also has a global twin. Collecting societies elsewhere in Africa, Kenya's among them, struggle with airplay monitoring ([Music In Africa](https://www.musicinafrica.net/magazine/broadcaster-royalties-kenya-still-huge-problem)), and AI-generated uploads now exceed half of Deezer's daily intake ([Deezer](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/07/ai-music-exceeds-50-percent-daily-uploads-deezer/)). The main risk is not the idea but the logistics. Two days before the festival, the official hackathon page still lists no dates, rules, criteria or AI policy, so the team must confirm these with organizers immediately.

## Four candidates scored: the usage ledger wins on every axis

Cameroon's music economy has a sharp, current, quantified failure. SONACAM's payouts fell from about 110M FCFA (2022) to 80M (2023) to a 40M FCFA envelope (2025), according to a specialist blog's compilation ([Muzisecur](https://www.muzisecur.fr/blog/vivre-de-sa-musique-cameroun-revenus-artiste/)). Eboa Lotin's son publicly reported receiving **2,000 FCFA**, after the board had promised 30,000–300,000 ([allAfrica](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202609230156.html)). Cameroon's whole distribution, roughly €61k, is a rounding error beside the **€90M** African societies collected in 2024 ([CISAC](https://www.cisac.org/cisac-global-collections-report-2025)). Kenya shows the failure is regional: there, inconsistent payouts are tied to irregular airplay monitoring ([Music In Africa](https://www.musicinafrica.net/magazine/broadcaster-royalties-kenya-still-huge-problem)). Meanwhile Cameroonian artists cannot use the YouTube Partner Program, and Spotify pays nothing on tracks under 1,000 annual streams ([Muzisecur](https://www.muzisecur.fr/blog/vivre-de-sa-musique-cameroun-revenus-artiste/)). Local airplay and live use are therefore the income that most needs measuring. Four candidates came out of the research. We scored them 1–5 against CIMFEST's known criteria plus presentation, which judging guides weight at about 15% ([Builder's Guide](https://intercom.help/builderbase/en/articles/14836129-builder-s-guide-ch-15-how-judging-works)). **The scores are our recommendation, not a sourced rubric.**

**Candidate A, TamTam Ledger (a verifiable usage network).** Phone listening nodes and radio-capture workers fingerprint what plays. A hash-chained public log records detections against a catalogue keyed by ISRC/ISWC (identifiers in the [DDEX](https://ddex.net) ecosystem). A distribution engine then splits the crate-levy pot and broadcast fees by measured plays and simulates mobile-money payouts. **Candidate B, an AI provenance and attribution registry for African repertoire**, is an "African STIM". Releases are signed with AudioSeal watermarks and C2PA manifests, and AI-generated uploads get a fingerprint-plus-embedding "nearest influences" report against a makossa and bikutsi catalogue. It matters because Deezer reports AI tracks exceed 50% of daily uploads and that **up to 70–85% of streams on fully AI tracks were fraudulent** ([Deezer](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/04/ai-generated-tracks-represent-44-of-new-uploaded-music/)). **Candidate C, a Living Archive**, would ingest field recordings of the kind held abroad, for example CREM-CNRS bikutsi recordings or 1907–1909 South Cameroon cylinders ([CREM](https://archives.crem-cnrs.fr/archives/items/CNRSMH_I_2016_021_004_025); [Fürniss](https://halshs.archives-ouvertes.fr/halshs-02401385v1/file/Fu%CC%88rniss%202016%20Convergences.pdf)). It would separate stems with [Demucs](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs), classify rhythm families and attach community-consent metadata. **Candidate D, offline-verifiable signed festival tickets with a mobile-money wallet**, would make screenshots of static QR codes worthless ([Bitdefender](https://www.bitdefender.com/blog/hotforsecurity/fake-ticket-scams/)), but the research found no Cameroon data on ticket fraud.

| **A. TamTam Ledger** | 4 | 5 | 5 | 5 | **19** | Needs SONACAM or venue buy-in to deploy |

Candidate A wins for three reasons. First, the jury already knows the problem: the 27,000 FCFA flat payout caused public outrage, and the crate levy is a July 2026 headline. Second, the central component, landmark fingerprinting of phone-mic recordings in a noisy room, is mature and demonstrable live. The research rates indexing a few hundred tracks and matching phone-mic queries "very feasible" in 48 hours. Third, it answers "long-term industry impact" directly, because measured usage is the prerequisite for usage-based royalties, for revenue-based financing of artists, and for any future AI licensing regime. We recommend folding **B's best idea into A as a stretch module**: the same fingerprint and embedding index flags AI soundalikes of registered works. That keeps the frontier AI angle without betting the demo on it. Candidate A also sidesteps the excluded framing entirely. Its pitch is "the money exists and nobody can split it fairly", not "connectivity is bad".

## TamTam Ledger turns phones into auditable royalty evidence

Everything in this section is our **recommendation**, built on the sourced building blocks above. The aim is a system design the team can defend component by component.

### Seven components with one-way data flow

The **edge listener node** is a web app or PWA on a cheap Android phone, placed in a bar, at a festival stage or beside an FM radio. It captures short audio windows on a randomized schedule, computes Shazam-style spectral-peak landmark hashes **on the device**, and discards the raw audio. Each batch of hashes becomes a "play observation" (node ID, timestamp, hash set) signed with the node's Ed25519 key. **Radio-capture workers** do the same from stream URLs or a phone held beside a radio. The **ingest API** checks signatures, rate limits and replay nonces, then queues observations. The **catalogue registry** stores works keyed by ISRC/ISWC, each with a split sheet (performer, composer, publisher shares) and a fingerprint index built with audfprint or Panako, which tolerates pitch shift and time stretch. The **matcher** looks up hashes in an inverted index and votes on time-offset histograms. It emits a detection only above a confidence threshold, and tags each detection with its confidence and the version of the matcher that produced it.

Detections go into the **transparency log**. This is an append-only Merkle tree with signed tree heads, in the style of Certificate Transparency. Any artist can fetch an **inclusion proof** that their play is in the log, and any auditor can check that the log was only ever appended to. The **distribution engine** is a pure, deterministic function: (log snapshot, pot amount, rules version) → per-rightsholder statement. It weights venues by sampling design, for example by crate volume per venue, attaches confidence intervals, and emits payouts to a simulated MTN MoMo / Orange Money rail. The **public dashboard and artist statement** view shows plays by region, venue type and work. A "Verify" button re-checks inclusion proofs in the browser.

The data flow runs in one direction: capture → hash → sign → ingest → match → log → distribute → statement. Nothing downstream can rewrite anything upstream.

### Six design decisions to explain to judges

| Decision | Chosen | Rejected alternative | Trade-off to explain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identification method | **Landmark fingerprinting** | Audio watermarking | Research found no watermark robust to speaker playback and re-recording ([SoK](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19176v2)), and re-encoding can strip watermarks ([AudioMarkBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06979v2)). Fingerprints need no cooperation from the label, but they need a catalogue. |
| Where matching happens | Hashes at the edge, matching centrally | Full matching on the phone | Raw audio never leaves the venue, so private conversations are never recorded. The central index can update instantly as artists register. |
| Integrity layer | **Merkle transparency log** | Public blockchain | The log gives the same tamper evidence with no tokens, gas or volatility. It is easier to explain to a collecting society, and tree heads can still be anchored publicly later. The notes themselves say a blockchain is unnecessary. |
| Coverage | **Stratified random sampling** of venues and time windows | Continuous census | Battery, cost and privacy favour sampling. The price is statistical uncertainty, which the engine reports as confidence intervals. Random windows also stop venues from "performing" for the sensor. |
| Distribution logic | **Deterministic, versioned rules** | Ad hoc spreadsheet or ML allocation | Any dispute can be replayed exactly. Rule changes are visible diffs, which answers the trust deficit left by the CMC/SOCAM/SONACAM conflicts ([Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/49423/en.html/droits-dauteur-la-nouvelle-affaire-sonacam)). |
| Anti-gaming | Signed nodes, per-node rate caps, loop and cluster anomaly detection, cross-node corroboration | Trusting reports | Faking plays to capture the pot is the obvious attack, and fraud is endemic in streaming (8% of all Deezer streams in 2025) ([Deezer](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/07/ai-music-exceeds-50-percent-daily-uploads-deezer/)). |

Two optional frontier hooks fit if time allows. The first is an **MCP server** over the catalogue and log, so any AI agent, DSP or broadcaster can query "who owns this and is it registered". It would use the newly stateless 2026-07-28 spec ([MCP blog](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28-release-candidate/)). The second is an **AI-soundalike flag** that compares new uploads against the catalogue using music embeddings, which the music research proposes as a retrieval-based proxy for Sureel-style attribution. Both are additions; neither is load-bearing.

### The wow moment is a live payout that anyone can audit

The demo should run in three beats, using songs from two or three CIMFEST artists who give written consent at the festival. Getting that consent is part of the adoption story. In the first beat, a teammate plays a makossa or bikutsi track through a speaker while a "bar noise" loop plays underneath. A phone on the judges' table identifies the track in seconds, and the dashboard map pulses in Buea. In the second beat, the presenter moves a slider for the "July 2026 crate levy: 100M FCFA". The artist's statement changes from the flat **27,000 FCFA** to a usage-based amount with a confidence interval. The presenter taps "Verify", and the inclusion proof checks green in the browser. In the third beat, a teammate edits one detection row directly in the database. The verifier turns red at once and names the broken tree head. That shows judges in ten seconds why the log can be trusted where past systems could not. A pre-recorded backup video of the full sequence is mandatory, because live demos fail and judges reward reliability over complexity ([Builder's Guide](https://intercom.help/builderbase/en/articles/14836129-builder-s-guide-ch-15-how-judging-works)).

### Presenting it as AI-built with human-owned architecture

Building openly with AI is now normal at winning events. CrossBeam's creator said he "didn't write a single line of code" ([Claude 4.6 winners](https://claude.com/blog/meet-the-winners-of-our-built-with-opus-4-6-claude-code-hackathon)). Some juries, though, penalize projects with no "meaningful contributions" from the team ([ETHGlobal](https://ethglobal.com/events/unite/info/details)), and a vibe-coding study found that human expertise still decided quality ([ICSE SEET 2026](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/510996/1/ICSE_SEET_2026_VibeCoding.pdf)). The team should therefore make the human architecture visible in four artefacts (our recommendation).

The first artefact is a **SPEC.md and CLAUDE.md** written by the team in the first three hours. They hold the domain rules (split-sheet semantics, pot rules, privacy constraints) and the interfaces between components, so that parallel Claude Code sessions build to a contract. The second is a set of **Architecture Decision Records** for each row of the decision table above. Each record is short, dated and signed by a human. The third is a **test pyramid**. Property-based tests on the distribution engine check conservation (payouts plus fees equal the pot exactly), determinism (the same inputs give the same statement) and monotonicity (more verified plays never lowers a share). A matching eval reports precision and recall at several signal-to-noise levels on phone recordings made in a real Buea bar. The fourth is a **deterministic simulation** in the TigerBeetle spirit ([TigerBeetle](https://tigerbeetle.com/blog/2026-08-20-protocol-aware-dst)). From one seed it simulates, for example, 200 venues over a week, with a known ground-truth playlist, injected noise and two injected frauds: a venue looping one song, and a cloned node key. It then reports allocation error against ground truth and whether each fraud was caught. The pitch then gets a Sim Francisco-style headline: "distribution within X% of ground truth; 2 of 2 injected frauds caught; seed 4417 reproduces it." The team should fill in X only with a measured result. A public repository with commit history ties all four artefacts together.

### A five-minute pitch built on one sentence

The one-sentence version is: "**Cameroon finally collects royalties from bars, but has no way to know whose music played, so TamTam turns any phone into tamper-proof evidence and pays artists by what was actually heard.**" Recommended flow: open with a real artist's payout (the 2,000 FCFA or 27,000 FCFA story) for about 20 seconds. Give the stakes in numbers for 20 seconds: 100M FCFA collected, no distribution key, €90M collected across Africa against Cameroon's tiny share. State the one-sentence solution, then run the three-beat live demo for about 90 seconds.
