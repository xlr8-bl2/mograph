# SoundProof should listen like Audoo, not Yacast

**Executive summary.** Very few collecting societies anywhere actually monitor what is played in bars, clubs and shops. Where they do, they split by venue type. **Clubs** are monitored with fixed boxes in a small random sample of venues, usually wired to the DJ mixer: GEMA's boxes sit "directly at the mixing desks", and Yacast's GEMA box "taps the output signal from the DJ booth" ([GEMA](https://www.gema.de/en/music-creators/monitoring/discotheque-monitoring)). **Bars, cafés and shops** are mostly paid on proxies such as radio and chart data, or on play logs from background-music providers like Soundtrack, which PRS will use from December 2026 ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/2026/10/28/prs-for-music-taps-soundtrack-for-better-public-performance-data/)). A few programmes put **microphone boxes in the room** with no connection to the sound system, and these are SoundProof's real precedents: Audoo's meters for PRS/PPL, APRA AMCOS, IMRO and a JASRAC trial; Monitor Latino's "discreet" capture devices for Colombia's OSA and Mexico's SACM; and Brazil's ECAD, whose staff fix recorders in venues ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/industry/prs-for-music-ppl-audoo-meters/); [Monitor Latino](https://www.monitorlatino.com/monitoreo-de-establecimientos/); [ECAD](https://www4.ecad.org.br/faq/como-o-ecad-sabe-quais-musicas-sao-tocadas/)). No African society runs in-venue recognition today, and SONACAM's method is not documented at all. The headline accuracy figures (Yacast 97%, DJ Monitor "up to 95%", Monitor Latino "99%") are self-reported, and most come from line-in feeds with human clean-up. The best independent evidence for microphone capture in real bars is lower but usable. On real phone and recorder audio from bars, clubs and concert halls, the open-weights **NMFP** fingerprinter identifies the track **84.6%** of the time from a 10-second clip, and **75%** from 2 seconds ([arXiv 2506.22661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.22661)). SoundProof should therefore build an Audoo-style box: a fixed microphone, fingerprints computed on the device, no audio leaving the venue. It should use an NMFP-class neural engine, retrained on Cameroonian bar noise. A play-logging layer should accept a song only when many consecutive windows agree, and the team should publish its own measured accuracy instead of quoting vendors. At CIMFEST that becomes a Docker demo: a laptop microphone and several simulated meters feed one matcher, one play log, one tamper-evident ledger and one payout statement, and the pitch quotes accuracy numbers the team measured during the 48 hours.

How to read this report: statements with a citation are **facts** from a source. Statements labelled *vendor claim* come from the company selling the product and have not been independently checked. Statements labelled *recommendation* or *inference* are our own engineering and strategy judgements built on those facts.

## What we will say to the group

"We're building an Audoo-style microphone meter, not a mixer tap. Europe's club systems plug into the DJ booth, but our bars play from phones, USB sticks and Bluetooth speakers. There's often no booth to plug into, and asking a bar owner to let us rewire his sound system is a dead end. Brazil, Colombia, Mexico, Japan and the UK already monitor venues with boxes that just listen to the room, so the approach is proven. What isn't proven is the accuracy: every number above 90% is a vendor talking about clean line-in audio. The best independent study on real bar and club microphone recordings gets about 85% per 10-second clip, and a 3–4 minute song gives us dozens of clips, so the play-level number should be much higher, provided we require consecutive clips to agree. We'll use the open NMFP neural fingerprinter, which was trained for exactly this, run it on the device so no conversation audio ever leaves the bar, and log a play only when the matches line up in time. For the hackathon we show one live laptop mic plus simulated meters in Docker, measure our own accuracy on the spot, put every play in a tamper-evident log anchored on-chain, and turn it into a payout statement. Our pitch line is that we don't trust vendor numbers, including our own, until they're measured."

## Societies monitor clubs by wire and bars by proxy

Recognition-based venue monitoring is narrow worldwide. In Europe it is concentrated in **discotheques and DJ-led festival stages**, run through a fixed box in a statistically chosen sample of roughly 80–120 venues per country. GEMA's Yacast era used about **120 boxes sampling one hour per week**. Since 1 January 2025, SoundAware (a Dutch GEMA subsidiary using SoundNodes hardware) monitors **80 clubs during all opening hours** under a five-year contract ([SoundAware](https://soundaware.com/2024/09/soundaware-to-monitor-music-in-german-clubs-for-gema-starting-in-2025/); [GEMA](https://www.gema.de/en/music-creators/monitoring/discotheque-monitoring)). GEMA's FY2023 fact sheet separately describes about 1,600 dance floors a year with 2 hours recorded per operating week, which suggests the boxes rotate through a much larger frame ([GEMA FY2023](https://www.gema.de/documents/d/guest/infoblatt_dk_dk_vr_2023_de-pdf)). Switzerland's SUISA runs "Hitboxes" in a static random sample that University of Zurich statisticians keep representative ([SUISA](https://www.suisa.ch/en/Musikschaffende/Verteilung-der-Einnahmen/Inland-Nutzungen/Musik-Monitoring-Clubs--Hitboxen-.html)). Spain's SGAE started with **90 venues** through BMAT in 2015–16, identifying about 500,000 plays in year one ([industriamusical](https://industriamusical.com/se-incrementa-el-numero-de-salas-en-espana-que-apuestan-por-la-tecnologia-de-identificacion-musical/)). The UK's PRS and PPL use DJ Monitor at clubs and festivals: in 2022 that covered 589 festival DJ sets and 25,000 club hours ([PRS](https://www.prsformusic.com/m-magazine/features/music-recognition-technology-mrt-djs-from-decks-to-data)). The United States is the clearest counter-example. ASCAP, BMI, SESAC and GMR send field investigators to bars for **enforcement, not distribution**, and no recognition monitoring was found ([WRAL](https://www.wral.com/listen-up-restaurants-pay-high-price-to-play-copyrighted-music/9984679/)).

Bars and shops sit lower on the data ladder. PRS ranks census, then sampling, then "analogy" (proxy) data, and names pub background music as a case where sampling "would not be cost effective" ([PRS policy](https://www.prsformusic.com/-/media/files/prs-for-music/membership/membership-policies/prs-distribution-policy/2prspolicydistributioncyclesandconceptsoct-22.pdf)). SOCAN pays venue money through radio pools ([SOCAN](https://www.socan.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Simplified-Distribution-Rules-2024.pdf)). The trend is toward direct data from background-music providers. PRS will receive play-by-play logs from more than **7,000 UK businesses** using Soundtrack from December 2026 ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/2026/10/28/prs-for-music-taps-soundtrack-for-better-public-performance-data/)), and Brazil's ECAD paid its first direct background-music distribution in January 2026 on **90 million plays in more than 3,500 stores** ([Mundo da Música](https://mundodamusicamm.com.br/ecad-mudanca-distribuicao-musica-ambiente/)). That route needs a B2B music provider in the shop. Cameroonian bars mostly play from DJs, phones, USB sticks and YouTube, so it does not apply there, and listening to the room becomes the realistic way to get data (*inference*).

The microphone precedents are fewer but real. **Audoo** meters are installed in "cafes, pubs, restaurants, retailers and other businesses" for PRS and PPL since August 2023 ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/2023/08/09/audoo-strikes-music-monitoring-deal-with-prs-for-music-and-ppl/)). Audoo's first commercial rollout was with APRA AMCOS, and IMRO in Ireland followed ([IMRO](https://imro.ie/industry-news/imro-partners-with-audoo-to-champion-innovation-and-elevate-music-monitoring-in-ireland/)). An Audoo–GEMA study recognised more than 130,000 tracks across hundreds of venues ([GEMA](https://www.gema.de/en/w/audoo-cooperation-music-impact-study)). JASRAC trialled the meter in Tokyo from December 2021, its first fingerprint-based collection of performance logs. The device "neither records nor sends audio" ([JASRAC](https://www.jasrac.or.jp/ejhp/release/2021/12-2.html); [AV Watch](https://av.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/1375218.html)). In Latin America, **Monitor Latino** sells "audio capture devices installed discreetly in each establishment" recording ambient audio 24/7, with OSA (Colombia) named as a user and SACM (Mexico) listed as a success case ([Monitor Latino](https://www.monitorlatino.com/monitoreo-de-establecimientos/)). **ECAD** has the longest-running programme in a comparable market. Its staff fix digital recorders in bars, restaurants, shops and live venues, and the recordings feed a statistical sample certified by Ibope ([ECAD FAQ](https://www4.ecad.org.br/faq/como-o-ecad-sabe-quais-musicas-sao-tocadas/); [ECAD Distribuição](https://www4.ecad.org.br/distribuicao/)). In Africa, monitoring stops at broadcast. BURIDA (Côte d'Ivoire) signed BMAT for broadcast in 2017 and piloted software in five Yopougon maquis and nightclubs in 2011 ([docplayer](https://docplayer.fr/116050936-Etude-technique-sur-les-droits-d-auteur.html); [Abidjan.net](https://news.abidjan.net/articles/406256/pose-de-stickers-teste-de-logiciel-dans-les-espaces-sonorises-a-yopougon-vers-le-paiement-mensuel-des-droits-dauteur)). ZAMCOPS (Zambia) uses ACRCloud for radio ([ACRCloud](https://www.acrcloud.com/zamcops/)). Nigeria's MCSN trialled GoCreate's "Go Monitor" ([Vanguard](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2022/01/gocreate-africa-unveils-music-monitoring-app-in-ghana/)). SONACAM paid a flat **27,000 FCFA** advance per beneficiary in October 2025 ([Mimi Mefo Info](https://mimimefoinfos.com/la-sonacam-indigne-les-artistes-camerounais-avec-27-000-fcfa-davance-de-droits-dauteur/)) and collected **100M FCFA from bars** through a crate levy in July 2026, with no usage data reported ([Cameroon Tribune](https://www.cameroon-tribune.cm/article.html/79271/en.html/copyrights-windfall-sonacam-secures-landmark-100-million-fcfa)).

## Vendors split between cloud audio upload and on-device fingerprints

The commercial field breaks into three architectures. **Line-in club boxes** come from Yacast, SoundAware and probably DJ Monitor. DJ Monitor's capture method could not be confirmed from a full-text source. **Ambient microphone meters** come from Audoo, Monitor Latino and BMAT's box, though BMAT's FAQ does not say whether its box uses a microphone or line-in. **Metadata feeds** come from KUVO and Soundtrack. Within the microphone group the most important design split is where recognition happens. BMAT sends recorded audio "to our servers to analyse" ([BMAT FAQ](https://www.bmat.com/faq/)). Audoo first separates music from background noise, then fingerprints on the device, and says "no audio is ever stored on device, or transmitted from it" ([Audoo FAQs](https://audoo.com/resources/faqs); [MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/industry/prs-for-music-ppl-audoo-meters/)). For a market with expensive mobile data and a new privacy law, Audoo's model is the right one to copy (*recommendation*).

Three vendor-side lessons carry over. First, **cost decides adoption**. PPL's CIO estimates a rollout across tens or hundreds of thousands of venues could cost "millions per year", while noting that Raspberry Pi-class hardware puts a capture computer "well under £50" ([MBW](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/there-is-a-real-need-to-look-behind-the-marketing-hype-and-truly-understand-what-is-being-offered)). Chile's SCD refuses to monitor venues at all because it "would cost more than it collects" ([BioBio](https://www.biobiochile.cl/especial/reportajes/2021/01/18/el-monopolio-de-la-musica-los-cobros-que-scd-profovi-y-otras-imponen-a-locales-pubs-y-restaurants.shtml)). No vendor publishes per-device prices. Second, **secrecy breeds distrust**. GEMA's Yacast boxes were criticised because venues could not see when they recorded and the list of monitored clubs was not public ([netzpolitik.org](https://netzpolitik.org/2015/black-box-discotheken-monitoring-gema-kooperiert-mit-yacast-gti-entwickelt-track-genaues-system/)), and DJs complained the results did not reflect club play ([iRights](https://irights.info/artikel/gema-diskotheken-monitoring-remix-sampling/26911)). Third, **the reference catalogue limits accuracy more than the microphone does**. GEMA (2016) and SUISA both added free member uploads of sound files because unregistered tracks went unmatched ([gema-politik](https://gema-politik.de/gema-vereinfacht-club-und-diskotheken-monitoring-durch-uploadmoeglichkeit-von-musikwerken-fuer-urheber/); [SUISAblog](https://blog.suisa.ch/en/soundfile-upload-in-the-member-portal/)).

| Independent research, real re-recording | POLARIS 84.6%, NMFP 74.3%, audfprint 61–67%, Olaf 24.4%, Panako 2.1 **4.8%** | [arXiv 2609.14820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14820) | Laptop speaker to iPhone at 1.6 m in an apartment; single-author preprint |
| Independent research, speaker-to-mic with noise | BEATs transfer: 97.0 / 90.2 / 56.5% at 15 s for low / mid / high distortion; original NAFP 17.5% at high | [Nikou & Giannakopoulos](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.06070) | Real playback with competing noise |
| Independent research, DJ mixes | Landmark systems on real DJ mixes: ~0.89 accuracy, ~0.65 specificity | [JKU / Sonnleitner](https://www.jku.at/fileadmin/gruppen/173/Research/187_Paper.pdf) | "A hard challenge" |

The most honest outside voice is PPL's CIO, Mark Douglas. In PPL's own overnight office tests, detection on non-mainstream music was sometimes low enough "to question the accuracy of the assertions made about accuracy". Niche genres matched *worse*, contrary to vendor pitches, and tracks that merely sampled a recording were misreported as covers. Because recognition has no context, adverts and interstitials got logged too. His prescription is trials and parallel running before trusting any vendor ([MBW](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/there-is-a-real-need-to-look-behind-the-marketing-hype-and-truly-understand-what-is-being-offered)). Two conclusions follow (*inference*). The 95–99% figures should never be presented as achievable by a microphone in a loud Yaoundé bar. And the binding risk for SoundProof is **reference coverage of makossa, bikutsi, afrobeats and coupé-décalé**, not acoustics. No published study measures fingerprint accuracy on any of those genres.

The good news is that a monitoring box never has to decide from one clip. A 3–4 minute song yields roughly 18 ten-second windows, or several hundred overlapping 1-second embeddings. Google's Now Playing reports that the correct song matches "every embedding" in a sequence, while false positives typically match only one or two ([arXiv 1711.10958](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.10958)). Per-play recall should therefore sit well above the per-clip 85%. That is an inference: windows inside one noisy song are correlated, and no paper reports play-level recall in real bars ([arXiv 2506.22661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.22661)). It is the number SoundProof has to measure itself.

## NMFP plus sequence voting is the engine to build on

*Recommendation:* use **NMFP** as the primary engine. It is the only fingerprinter validated on real bar and club microphone audio with public code and weights (github.com/raraz15/neural-music-fp). It runs at 8 kHz with 1 s windows and a 0.5 s hop, and was built by UPF with BMAT and Sony AI ([arXiv 2506.22661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.22661)). Its storage cost is 3.54 MiB per hour of reference audio, and a 10 s query takes 0.33 s on an M2 Max CPU ([arXiv 2609.14820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14820)). Keep **audfprint** as a classic baseline the team can explain in one slide, and treat **POLARIS** (1.89 MiB/h, training-free, 84.6% on re-recordings) as a promising second voter to fuse with NMFP, not as the primary, because it is a single-author preprint with no independent check. Do **not** make Panako or Olaf the primary engine. At 4.8% and 24.4% on real re-recordings they are far behind ([arXiv 2609.14820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14820)). This also corrects the earlier TamTam Ledger plan, which proposed audfprint or Panako. For tempo- and pitch-shifted DJ play, **PeakNetFP** is open and stays above 98% top-1 at 10 s across 0.7–1.4× stretch with only 169k parameters, but it was tested only on stretching, not on noise ([arXiv 2506.21086](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21086v1)). Fine-tuned MuQ foundation-model fingerprints handle time stretch (96%) and pitch shift (94%) best, but release no weights and need a server ([arXiv 2511.05399](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05399)).

Training data matters more than architecture. In NMFP's ablation, four degradation changes plus false-negative removal lifted real-venue 1 s accuracy from **33.6% to 54.5%**: realistic scene noise, full-length room impulse responses (the acoustic "echo signature" of a room), reverberation carried over from the previous second, and a 160 Hz rather than 300 Hz low-frequency cutoff, because far from the speaker the bass is what survives the crowd. The chain is noise at 0–10 dB SNR, then room IR, then microphone IR, with rooms and mics kept separate between training and test. Triplet loss beat NT-Xent ([arXiv 2506.22661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.22661)). Other useful recipes add MUSAN restaurant noise, band-pass filters, 100–200 ms echo and Encodec codec artefacts ([arXiv 2511.05399](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05399)), or SpecAugment and small random time offsets, which their authors call "crucial" ([arXiv 2508.11609](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11609v2)). *Recommendation:* fine-tune NMFP on Cameroonian conditions after the hackathon. That means crowd chatter in French, English and Pidgin, football on TV, generator hum and street noise, plus impulse responses measured with a sine sweep through the PAs of a few real bars, plus the chosen microphone's own measured response. A few hours of aligned clean and bar-recorded pairs, the kind of supervision Now Playing used ([arXiv 1711.10958](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.10958)), would serve as both training data and a test set.

At test time, the biggest lever is the effective query length. *Recommendation:* fingerprint continuously at a 0.5 s hop and run a FAISS top-20 nearest-neighbour search per embedding. Then score candidate sequences by offset consistency, as NMFP and Now Playing do, and use stricter thresholds for obscure songs than for hits, following Sound Search's popularity weighting ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/googles-next-generation-music-recognition/)). Skip heavy source separation. A 2025 study found it substantially helps peak-based fingerprinting but gives "no substantial benefit" to neural fingerprints, and the separation checkpoints are around 600 MB ([UPF](https://repositori.upf.edu/items/ca89891c-f07a-4488-90b3-762aae96a53a/full); [GitHub](https://github.com/guillemcortes/ssafp)). Run it, if at all, on the server for unresolved segments when using audfprint.

## Mic placement decides signal-to-noise, so treat it as a protocol

Peer-reviewed evidence on microphone hardware for fingerprinting is thin. No study compares MEMS, USB condenser and directional mics, and none gives an accuracy-versus-distance curve for real venues. What is documented is that operating-system voice processing damages music. Android's `VOICE_COMMUNICATION` source applies echo cancellation and automatic gain control. `UNPROCESSED` (API 24+) gives raw audio where supported. `VOICE_RECOGNITION` must by default disable noise reduction and gain control and stay roughly flat (±3 dB) from 100 Hz to 4 kHz ([Android AudioSource](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/media/MediaRecorder.AudioSource); [Android CDD](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/compatibility/cdd/+show/refs/tags/android-platform-12.1.0_r10/5_multimedia/5_4_audio-recording.md)). Some USB mics expose an "Auto Gain Control" switch in the Linux ALSA mixer that must be turned off with `amixer` ([Home Assistant forum](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/atr4697-usb-mic-needs-alsamixer-to-set-auto-gain-control-how-to-on-home-assistant-os-on-pi5/824339)). The capture rate must match the model's training rate. GraFP collapsed when fed upsampled 8 kHz audio, and NMFP runs at 8 kHz, Conformers at 16 kHz ([arXiv 2506.22661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.22661)).

*Recommendation (engineering judgement, not benchmarked):* capture 16-bit audio at 16 kHz or 48 kHz and resample to the model's rate. Fix the gain by hand so PA peaks do not clip. Mount the meter 1–3 m from the main speaker and pointed at it, above head height, away from the bar counter and the crowd, using a cardioid USB mic if possible. Avoid speech-oriented beamforming arrays unless their AGC, noise suppression and echo cancellation can be bypassed. For the hackathon's laptop microphone in a browser, request audio with `echoCancellation`, `noiseSuppression` and `autoGainControl` set to false. Log the noise floor and input level with every batch, so the field calibration can model placement as a covariate. At installation, record placement and distance in a short checklist with a photo, and play a known test track to confirm detection before leaving the venue.

## DJ mixes need an offset-tracking play logger, not a song counter

The literature agrees that DJ mixes are hard. Sonnleitner et al. call DJ-mix monitoring "a hard challenge" for landmark systems ([UnmixDB](https://zenodo.org/records/1422385/files/schwarz-fourer-ismir2018late-breaking-unmixdb.pdf)). DJs play extended mixes, edits and remixes, so the catalogue must hold the exact versions played ([Kim et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.10267)). PPL itself says mixing and mashups make recognition harder ([PRS](https://www.prsformusic.com/m-magazine/features/music-recognition-technology-mrt-djs-from-decks-to-data)). The best published method for localising tracks inside mixes takes the top-5 neighbours per segment and drops those below 0.7 similarity. It then fits the reference time against the query time with Huber regression, so the slope captures tempo change, and keeps the trajectory with the most inliers. With MuQ embeddings that reached a boundary F1 of 74.7, against 58.4 for the Shazam-like Dejavu ([arXiv 2511.05399](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05399)). Cameroon's DJ-heavy nightclubs are the hard case, and its phone-and-USB bars are the easy one (*inference*). SoundProof should launch in bars and treat clubs as phase two.

*Recommendation:* the play logger is a state machine. For each candidate track it keeps a hypothesis of reference offset against wall-clock time. It **opens** a play when N matches within 10–20 s agree on offset and on a slope near 1, or a slope away from 1 when the DJ has pitched the track. It **extends** the play while matches continue, and **closes** it after 15–30 s with no consistent match. It logs start, end, duration, reference start and end offsets, slope and confidence. It merges hypotheses across short dropouts such as a crowd cheer, and logs a new play when the reference offset jumps back near zero. During crossfades it allows two concurrent hypotheses. It applies a minimum duration, for example 30 s, which must be checked against SONACAM's rules. Unmatched music windows go into an "unknown" bucket that feeds the escrow pool from the earlier estimation work, so unregistered artists are counted rather than ignored. A cheap music/no-music gate saves compute. Google's 8k-parameter detector reached 75.5% recall at one false trigger per 20 minutes ([arXiv 1711.10958](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.10958)), which is too leaky to gate a royalty system strictly. In bars, where music plays almost continuously, set it permissively or fingerprint on a fixed duty cycle.

## Fingerprint at the edge, match in Cameroon, store and forward

*Recommendation:* the box runs capture, the music gate and NMFP embedding on the device. It uploads compact, signed embedding batches, never audio, to a matching server hosted in Cameroon with a FAISS index and the play logger. When offline, it stores and forwards. The sizing arithmetic (*inference* from published per-hour costs) is favourable. A 30,000-track catalogue is about 1,750 hours of audio, or about 6.2 GB of float NMFP references. With the roughly 32× product-quantisation compression Now Playing used, that drops to about 200 MB, which fits on one CPU server or even on the device. Raw embedding upload is about 4 MB per hour of operation before compression ([arXiv 2609.14820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14820); [arXiv 1711.10958](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.10958)). No published Raspberry Pi benchmark exists for NMFP, PeakNetFP or the Conformers. A Pi 4 running a 1–9M-parameter int8 model at 1–2 inferences per second is very likely feasible but must be measured. An old Android phone is a credible alternative box. Now Playing ran continuous recognition for 0.9% of a Pixel 2's battery per day ([arXiv 1711.10958](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.10958)). Central matching beats full on-device matching for SoundProof, because the catalogue updates the moment an artist registers. Edge embedding beats BMAT-style audio upload, because no patron conversation ever leaves the bar.

Privacy is a design requirement, not a feature. Cameroon's Law 2024/017 became enforceable on 23 June 2026. It requires prior authorisation for processing and for transfers abroad, and reported fines reach 1 billion FCFA ([Droit Médias & Finance](https://droitmediasfinance.com/index.php/actualites/droit-tech-fintech/1297-cameroun-la-loi-sur-la-protection-des-donnees-a-caractere-personnel-entre-en-vigueur-ce-23-juin-2026); [Digital Business Africa](https://www.digitalbusiness.africa/cameroun-bientot-des-peines-damende-d-un-milliard-de-francs-cfa-pour-violation-des-donnees-personnelles/)). Monitor Latino's "discreet" installation and Yacast's hidden recording schedule are what *not* to copy. *Recommendation:* sign a venue consent contract, put up a visible notice and an LED on the box, give each venue owner a dashboard of their own logged plays, host data in Cameroon, and register with the data protection authority before any pilot. Keep the *timing* of audits and the panel membership confidential to resist fraud. Being open about the device itself is compatible with that.

## Field evaluation: measure the box before quoting a number

*Recommendation*, adapting the NMFP, SD-RR and Now Playing protocols ([arXiv 2506.22661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.22661); [arXiv 2609.14820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14820); [arXiv 1711.10958](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.10958)) and the earlier planted-play calibration:

| Phase | What | Ground truth | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Bench (week 1) | Genre-stratified local tracks played through a PA; recorded at 1 / 3 / 5 / 10 m with bar noise at +10 / +5 / 0 / −5 dB SNR | Sync chirps align recording to playlist | Per-window top-1 at 2 / 5 / 10 s by genre, distance, SNR |
| 2. Controlled venues | 5–10 bars of varied size and loudness, daytime and evening; known playlist via the venue's own system; candidate mics and placements side by side | Chirps plus a loopback or line recording where the owner allows | Per-play recall and precision; best placement |
| 3. Planted plays | Staff play a randomised list of known tracks monthly, full length and as snippets | The planted list | Detection probability *p̂* with Wilson interval per venue |
| 4. Shadow logging | 2–4 weeks per venue; auditor logs a sample of nights by hand or the DJ exports a set list | Manual log / DJ export | Live recall, precision, boundary error |
| 5. Open-set distractors | Hours of non-catalogue music, speech-only and TV audio | Known negatives | **False plays per venue-hour** |
| 6. Two-meter recapture | Two meters in different positions | None needed | Drift alarm (biased low, so not a replacement for planted plays) |

Report three levels. The first is per window, at 2, 5 and 10 s. The second is per play: recall of plays at least 30 s long, precision of logged plays, start and end error in seconds, duration error and duplicate rate. The third is royalty impact: misallocated FCFA from the share estimator. Count "no answer" as wrong. Weight tracks uniformly, because NMFP found that some tracks appeared 11 times in a widely used benchmark while others were absent. Keep test venues and devices separate from any used for fine-tuning. Publish the results with intervals. Independent certification, along the lines of ECAD's Ibope-certified sample ([ECAD](https://www4.ecad.org.br/distribuicao/)), by a Cameroonian university statistics department would turn SoundProof's openness into legitimacy that SONACAM currently lacks.

## What to build at CIMFEST in 48 hours

The judging weights favour a working, honest system over breadth. Problem fit (25%) and working demo (25%) together decide half the score. *Recommendation:* map every build item to a criterion and cut anything that maps to none.

| Criterion (weight) | What earns it | Build item |
|---|---|---|
| Problem fit (25%) | The 27,000 FCFA flat payout and the 100M FCFA levy with no usage data; bars with no mixer to plug into | Artist statement: flat vs usage-based, with interval |
| Working demo (25%) | A live laptop mic identifying a local track over bar noise, in seconds, end to end to a payout | Listener → matcher → play logger → ledger → statement |
| Viability (20%) | Sub-£50 hardware class, sampled panel, privacy by design, cost per venue against the 100M pot | Simulated meter fleet + cost slide + privacy slide |
| Technical execution (15%) | Measured accuracy, offset-consistent play logging, signed batches, tamper evidence, tests | Eval harness, property tests, Merkle log |
| Pitch (15%) | One sentence, one live moment, honest numbers | Rehearsed 3-beat demo plus backup video |

The architecture is one `docker compose up`. A **listener** service captures the laptop microphone (browser `getUserMedia` with processing disabled, or Python), computes embeddings and uploads signed batches with no audio. A **meter simulator** spins up N containers, each acting as a bar. Each replays catalogue tracks through the NMFP-style degradation chain (scene noise at 0–10 dB, room IR, mic IR) or replays bar recordings the team makes in Buea during the event, following a seeded playlist with known ground truth. It also injects outages and two frauds: a looped song and a cloned meter key. An **ingest** service verifies Ed25519 signatures and nonces. A **matcher** holds a FAISS index over 50–300 consented tracks. A **play logger** runs the state machine above. A **ledger** keeps an append-only Merkle log of plays with inclusion proofs. A **distribution engine** computes sample-weighted shares and the payout statement. A **dashboard** shows the live map, plays, measured accuracy and the "Verify" button.

Two hackathon constraints change the plan from earlier research. The first is that **all core code must be written during the 48 hours**. The team should confirm with organisers on day one that third-party libraries and published pretrained weights (FAISS, librosa, the NMFP checkpoint) count as dependencies, not core code. The play logger, matcher glue, ledger, simulator, estimator and UI are the team's core and are written on site. As a fallback, a Shazam-style landmark fingerprinter is small enough to write during the event, and it works well for a speaker-to-laptop demo in a quiet hall, though not for real bars. That gap is itself a slide: *"Our 48-hour engine versus the real-venue engine we'll pilot."* Write no code before the event. Specs and decision records drafted in the first hours are fine. The second constraint is that **Africa Blockchain Festival and Web3Bridge are co-organisers**. *Recommendation:* keep the Merkle log off-chain, but anchor each signed tree head, and the hash of each payout statement, to a public testnet with a few lines of contract code. That gives web3 judges a verifiable on-chain checkpoint and music judges a plain story. Use no token, because a token adds risk and nothing the problem needs.

| Hours | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Confirm rules (dependencies, pretrained weights, AI use); SPEC.md, interfaces, decision records; collect 2–3 artist consents and tracks |
| 3–12 | Listener + index + matcher returning a correct ID from the laptop mic; Docker skeleton |
| 12–20 | Play-logger state machine; signed ingest; Merkle log with inclusion proofs |
| 20–28 | Meter simulator with seeded ground truth and fraud injection; record 20–30 minutes in a real Buea bar at 2–3 distances |
| 28–34 | Eval harness: per-window and per-play recall/precision, false plays per hour; distribution engine with property tests; on-chain anchor |

The demo has three beats. First, a makossa track plays through a speaker over a bar-noise loop, the laptop identifies it, a play opens on the dashboard and closes when the song ends. Second, the simulated fleet's 48-hour results appear: measured recall and false plays per venue-hour, frauds caught, and the levy slider moving an artist from 27,000 FCFA to a usage-based amount with a range. Third, a teammate edits one play row, the verifier turns red, and the on-chain tree head shows that the record was changed.

## Conclusion

The research changes how SoundProof should frame its constraint. Not plugging into the sound system sounded like a handicap next to Europe's line-in club boxes. It turns out to be the mainstream approach for exactly the venues Cameroon has: Audoo, Monitor Latino, ECAD and JASRAC all chose microphones because bars and shops have no booth to wire into. The real frontier is not capture. It is **independent proof of accuracy**, which no vendor has published, and **catalogue coverage of local repertoire**, which no global database provides. A team that ships an open engine, a play-level metric and a field protocol is arguing from evidence, which the incumbents' marketing is not.

That reframing also sets the scope. The hackathon cannot prove real-bar accuracy in 48 hours, and it should not pretend to. It can prove that the full chain works, from a microphone to a tamper-evident payout. It can show that the team measures instead of claiming, and that the one number nobody else publishes, play-level recall with false plays per venue-hour in a Cameroonian bar, is something SoundProof is set up to produce in a pilot. The rule for the pitch is that any accuracy number on a slide must be one the team measured during the event.
